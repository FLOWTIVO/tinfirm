# Security review of the platform, 2026-10-27

Scope: this repository on `main` as of 2026-10-27, its reusable CI workflows and composite actions included. Reviewed at the owner's request, as a code review: the code of each trust boundary was read end to end and held to the threat model's claims for that boundary and to the accepted risks (threat-model §7), and every claim was verified; the code behind every High and Medium finding was checked a second time before the finding was kept. Nothing was executed against a running instance except two throw-away checks (the Markdown renderer, M10; the migrations applied to a scratch PostgreSQL for the RLS table). No test suite was run. Nothing was changed. It follows the review whose 18 findings [2026-10-27-review-findings-fixed.md](2026-10-27-review-findings-fixed.md) records as fixed; none of the findings below repeats one of those. The fixes, the accepted risks and what stays open are recorded in [2026-10-27-security-review-fixed.md](2026-10-27-security-review-fixed.md), following [the plan](2026-10-27-security-review-plan.md).

How to read it: a finding is numbered by severity (H, M, L, I), names the files and lines, states who can do what, and gives the fix. Section 5 lists what was checked and found sound, which is most of the platform. Section 6 lists what the review could not see. Severity follows the threat model's own scale: High is a privilege escalation within a company, an auth bypass with preconditions or a secret disclosure within scope; Medium is a defence-in-depth gap with a realistic path, a low-effort denial of service, or a disclosure of limited value.

## 1. Summary

The platform's security design is unusually explicit and most of it is implemented as described: the vault, the audit chain, the session bundles, tenant isolation at the database (96 of 114 tables under forced row-level security, no cross-tenant path found), the outbound guard, the runner host's sandbox and its three proxies, the inbound signatures of Slack, Teams, the webhook and SCIM, the web app's sinks, and the CI pinning and image hardening all held up under a hostile reading. No Critical finding.

Four High findings, each a logic gap in an identity or authority rule rather than a missing primitive:

- **H1.** SCIM provisioning plus first sign-in lets one company's identity provider take over a user that another company's SCIM created and who has not signed in yet. Cross-tenant account takeover on a multi-company instance.
- **H2.** An ADMIN can reassign any trigger to any member, name that member's own accounts and budget in its table, and run it: the admin acts with the member's (the OWNER's included) personal GitHub, Jira, Slack and MCP accounts without the member's consent or notice.
- **H3.** The GitHub App key that pushes past `main`'s ruleset is an ordinary repository secret, and the release signing runs on the same runners as pull-request code. Write access to any branch reaches `main` without review, an auto-deploy host and, with a concurrent release, the image signing key.
- **H4.** A SAML provider's entity ID, the key of every user's identity, is taken from the provider's own metadata document and never pinned. On an instance with several SAML providers, whoever controls one provider's metadata can publish another provider's entity ID and sign in as that provider's users.

Eighteen Medium findings follow, of which M11 and M12 are withdrawn. The others, grouped by theme: the model relay forwards providers' server-side tools (a tunnel out of the sandbox), runner-facing endpoints lack flood controls, two kill-switch and role-change gaps, the MCP OAuth discovery trusts endpoints on any origin, the Markdown renderer lets an attribute-bearing tag through, two more first-sign-in binding gaps, the reusable CI workflows pin every action by a mutable tag, and the release assets are verified only by a checksum beside them.

What to fix first: H1, H4, M16 (identity binding: a few lines each); H2, M6 and M7 (authority gaps, small code changes); H3, M18 and M13 (the release and its CI); M1 and M8 (one allow-list each); then the flood controls (M3–M5).

## 2. Findings: High

### H1. A company's SCIM and identity provider can take over a user another company provisioned

- **Where:** `server/src/main/java/com/flowtivo/tinfirm/identity/internal/ExternalProvisioning.java:455-475` (`scimUser`); `ee/src/main/java/com/flowtivo/tinfirm/ee/scim/ScimDirectoryImpl.java:176-181` (`create`); `company/internal/ProvisionedMembershipsImpl.java:187-189`; `identity/internal/UserEntity.java:112-115`.
- **What:** `POST /scim/v2/Users` in company A adopts any existing user of the instance by email (by design, ADR-0087 decision 7) and writes A's `externalId` onto A's membership. A first sign-in through A's provider binds the identity to the SCIM user whose `externalId` matches, filtered only by `authSource == SCIM && active && !ownsACompany`. `app_user.scim_company_id`, which V062 documents as "only that company's IdP may bind the user", is stored but never consulted at binding.
- **Who and what:** an OWNER of company A on a multi-company instance, holding A's SCIM token and controlling A's identity provider, with a user of company B that B's SCIM created and who has not yet signed in. A adopts the user with an `externalId` of A's choosing, then signs in with that subject: the session is the victim's, with the victim's membership and role in B. The victim is then locked out (auth source is now OIDC with A's issuer). The `EXTERNAL_ID_OR_EMAIL` variant needs only an asserted email.
- **Fix:** in `scimUser`, require `companyId.equals(user.getScimCompanyId())`; in `ScimDirectoryImpl.create`, refuse (409) to adopt a user awaiting sign-in whose `scim_company_id` is another company, or ignore the caller's `externalId` for adopted users. Add a test that provisions through B's SCIM, adopts through A's, and signs in through A's provider.
- **Docs:** contradicts threat-model 17.2 and 17.4 and the intent of ADR-0087 decision 6. Not an accepted risk. The same adoption by email (also `MemberService.add`) is the mechanism that matters whenever companies on one instance do not trust each other.

### H2. An ADMIN can run any member's own accounts and budget through a reassigned trigger

- **Where:** `server/src/main/java/com/flowtivo/tinfirm/trigger/internal/TriggerService.java:1407-1475` (`reassign`), `:591-644` (`choices` with `forPrincipalId`); `TriggerCallers.java:71-76` (`reassigns = admin`); `TriggerStore.reassign` (clears `admin_changed_*`); `trigger/TriggerEvents.java:49` (`TriggerReassigned` has no listener).
- **What:** OWNER and ADMIN may reassign any live trigger to any active member at any time; nothing limits it to a trigger whose author left (`AUTHOR_LEFT`). The request's `accounts` and `payer` are checked only against what the new author is entitled to, so the admin may fill the table with the member's own accounts and make the member's budget the payer. `GET /triggers/choices?forPrincipalId=<any member>` lists that member's own account ids to any admin. The reassignment pauses the trigger, but the same admin may edit the instruction and the agent "on the author's behalf", accept widenings, resume and run it. The new author receives no inbox item (the `admin_changed_*` note is erased by `reassign`) and nothing listens to `TriggerReassigned`.
- **Who and what:** an ADMIN against any MEMBER or the OWNER. The trigger's conversations act as the member at GitHub, Jira, Slack and the member's personal MCP servers (added through the trigger's settings), spend the member's budget, and the admin reads the conversations and outputs as an admin of every trigger. The same path adds the member's personal MCP servers, since `saveSettings` resolves `ownServers` for the author.
- **Fix:** allow reassignment only of `AUTHOR_LEFT` or `NOT_ENTITLED` triggers; keep a reassigned trigger paused until the new author accepts its rows, payer and open-events acknowledgement (reset `open_events_acknowledged_at`), and notify them; when an admin edits on the author's behalf, refuse rows that name the author's own accounts or MCP servers unless the author set them; limit `choices?forPrincipalId=` to the author of the trigger being edited.
- **Docs:** contradicts PLATFORM §4 (ADMIN's rights do not include members' accounts), §5.2 (a member's secret is revealed only for a call that acts as them, which they chose) and §7.2 (the acknowledgement is the author's). Threat-model 8.36 names "admin edits on the author's behalf" as a mitigation; here it is the vector. CONVENTIONS §15.15 documents the behaviour, so this is a design gap, not a coding error.

### H3. Deployment keys reachable from any branch, and signing on shared runners

Confidence Medium: the workflow facts are verified; the GitHub organisation settings (secret scoping, runner groups, branch rulesets) were not visible to the review and could narrow it.

- **Where:** `.github/workflows/release.yml:112-123` (job `release`: `RELEASE_APP_*` from `secrets.*` with no `environment:`); `pr_agent.yml:86` (`OPENAI_KEY`); `release.yml:83-96,158-200` (`signing-key` and `sign` on the runner label of pull-request jobs); `Dockerfile:27,70`, `model-api/Dockerfile:15`, `deploy/release/runner-musl.Dockerfile:56-57` (BuildKit cache ids shared by every job); `.github/CODEOWNERS` (covers only `release.yml`, `sign-images.sh`, `cosign.pub`, licence keys).
- **What:** (a) The release App's key, which bypasses `main`'s "pull request required" ruleset, is a repository or organisation secret. A workflow file on any same-repository branch runs with it, and `main`'s branch protection does not guard a workflow that runs from a feature branch; conditions such as `if: push to main` are in the file the branch controls. The branch's `permissions:` can also give `GITHUB_TOKEN` `contents: write` (replace the `rolling` assets, which an auto-deploy host installs within five minutes, see M13) or `packages: write` (re-push a `sha-*` image or chart that a deployment pulls by mutable tag). (b) `signing-key` and `sign` run on the same runners as same-repository pull-request jobs (`./mvnw verify`, `npm ci` with lifecycle scripts, `docker build` of the PR's own Dockerfile). The cosign key and password are process environment on that runner, and, on a runner that persists between jobs, the PR's `docker build` writes the `tinfirm-m2` and `tinfirm-npm` cache mounts the release build reads. There is no transparency log (ADR-0081).
- **Who and what:** anyone with push access to a branch, or a malicious dependency pulled by a PR build: commit to `main` without review, replace the artefacts an auto-deploy host installs, and with timing, sign images that every customer's `cosign verify` accepts.
- **What the reusable workflows show:** they mint installation tokens narrowed to this repository and revoke them when the job ends, and a genuine release is gated by `signing-key`'s `environment: release`; the gap is where the private keys are stored and which runners read them, not how they are used. A branch workflow need not call the reusable workflow at all to read the App's private key.
- **Fix:** environments with deployment-branch rules are the control GitHub offers against a branch workflow reading a secret, and a reusable workflow's job may take `environment:` from an input: give the reusable release workflow's `tag` and `next` jobs an `environment` input and read `RELEASE_APP_*` from the caller's `release` environment; scope `OPENAI_KEY` to an environment restricted to `main` with no reviewers. Extend CODEOWNERS to `.github/**`, `deploy/**`, the Dockerfiles and `dependabot.yml`; tag rulesets for `rolling` and `v*`; run `signing-key`, `sign` and the release image build on a runner label pull-request jobs cannot use (or ephemeral runners), with their own cache ids; `npm ci --ignore-scripts` where scripts are not needed; consider keyless or KMS-held signing.
- **Docs:** ADR-0081 ("the key lives only in the `release` environment, behind its reviewers") and threat-model 6.3 "Built" overstate: the environment gate covers the secret, not the runner. R53 (unsigned `main`/`rolling` images) assumes an attacker without merge rights cannot reach them.

### H4. A SAML provider's entity ID is taken from its remote metadata and never pinned

- **Where:** `ee/src/main/java/com/flowtivo/tinfirm/identity/internal/saml/SamlIdpMetadata.java:160,207` (the entity ID comes from the document; `_ENTITY_ID` only filters a multi-entity document when set); `SamlRegistrations.java:143` (the registration's asserting party is `idp.entityId()`); `SamlLoginImpl.java:105` (that value is the identity's issuer); `server/.../identity/internal/SamlProperties.java:120` (`_ENTITY_ID` optional); `ExternalProvisioning.java:312` (the user is found by `(issuer, subject)` only, never by registration); `SamlMetadataRefresh.java:162-182` (a refresh audits a change of keys, not of entity ID).
- **What:** nothing ties a registration to a fixed entity ID, nothing refuses two providers that share one, and a change of entity ID at refresh is neither refused nor audited. Threat-model 16.9 covers the keys only; ADR-0086 decision 5 and CONVENTIONS §5 assume the entity ID names one provider.
- **Who and what:** whoever administers, or can change the metadata document of, one declared SAML provider whose `_ENTITY_ID` is unset, on an instance with more than one SAML provider: typically another company's self-hosted IdP. They publish the other provider's entity ID, sign assertions with their own keys (which the registration trusts, since they come from the same document), and their sign-ins resolve to the other provider's existing users by subject: account takeover across companies. The same move squats `(issuer, subject)` pairs so the real users get `identity_in_use`.
- **Fix:** require `_ENTITY_ID` on every SAML provider, or store the first accepted entity ID and refuse any later document that names another (audited, shown on the sign-in page); refuse to start when two providers, or a SAML provider and an OIDC issuer, share an identifier; consider adding the registration id to the identity key.

## 3. Findings: Medium

### M1. The model relay forwards providers' server-side tools, a tunnel out of the sandbox

- **Where:** `libs/litellm/src/main/java/com/flowtivo/tinfirm/shared/litellm/LiteLlmRequests.java:49-104` (the removed-field list: LiteLLM's own fields only; `tools`, `mcp_servers` and `container` pass); `model/internal/ModelRelayService.java:132-133` (`anthropic-beta` and `openai-beta` forwarded).
- **What:** a prompt-injected agent whose sandbox is held by the egress allowlist can send, through the model proxy and the relay, a request that asks the provider to call an attacker's server: OpenAI Responses `tools: [{type: "mcp", server_url: ...}]` or `web_search`; Anthropic `mcp_servers: [{type: "url", url: ...}]` with the matching beta header, or `web_fetch`/`web_search`. The provider's infrastructure then carries whatever the agent puts in the arguments to any host. Whether LiteLLM 1.106 forwards each of these to each provider was not tested; the code gap is verified.
- **Fix:** in the relay's body filter drop `mcp_servers` and `container`; allow only client-side `tools[].type` values (function, custom, the runtimes' local types) and refuse server-tool types; allow-list the beta header values; optionally a company setting that permits web search.
- **Docs:** materially worse than R13 and threat-model 7.2 ("the model provider is still a channel"): the provider becomes a relay to any host, which defeats the TB7 allowlist.

### M2. A personal host can receive a group-lent credential's git token (race)

- **Where:** `run/internal/RunDispatcher.java:366,394` (host chosen from `TurnSource.plan`'s holders); `RunDelivery.java:181` (content built later, outside the dispatch transaction); `conversation/internal/TurnSourceImpl.java:214-262` (`content` re-reads the workspace and reveals a token per repository through `bindings.use`; `placement.hostKind()` is received and never used).
- **What:** the rule "a personal host runs only work whose every git account is its owner's own" (threat-model 5.14) is checked in `plan()` and not again at reveal. A `REPOSITORY` approval commits at once and is not blocked by an assigned run, so a repository bound through a group grant can join the workspace between the eligibility check and the reveal, and `run.assign` then carries the company credential's token to the member's machine. A READ grant's token can still write; `readOnly` is a flag for the host.
- **Who and what:** a member with a personal host and a group grant on a code host, timing an approval against their own run's dispatch; one attempt per approvable repository.
- **Fix:** in `content()`, when the placement is `PERSONAL`, recompute the holders of the repositories about to be revealed and take the run back if any is not the payer; or reveal only the repository ids `plan()` approved.

### M3. The model relay reserves twice the declared body before reading it

- **Where:** `model/internal/ModelRelayService.java:596-611` (`share.add(2 * length)` then `readNBytes`); defaults 64 MiB per body, 256 MiB per replica (`ModelProperties`).
- **What:** the replica's shared in-flight budget is taken up front, and the thread then waits for the body. Two requests from any host with one running budget-paid run (a member's personal host qualifies), declaring 64 MiB and sending a byte every 50 seconds, hold the whole budget: every other company's model requests on the replica fail with 429 and their runs fail. `model-api/.../ModelPort.java:140` counts bytes as they arrive; the relay does not.
- **Fix:** use the incremental read for declared lengths too, and put a deadline on reading the body.

### M4. Runner-facing frames have no flood controls

- **Where:** `run/internal/RunRequestRouter.java:125-187` (an unbounded virtual thread per `run.request`); `conversation/internal/ToolCalls.java:125-170` (three to five transactions, a `tool_call` row, an audit row and an external call per tool call); `runner/RunnerMessages.java:672-674` and `run/internal/RunnerSessionHandler.java:340-370` (`run.event` payload unbounded; the 256 KiB cap of the schema is host-enforced only; 20 000 events per run of up to 1 MiB each); `ConversationWorkers` and `RunWorkers` (unbounded executors); the pool is 10 connections per pod.
- **What:** a malicious host, or a prompt-injected agent through the host's MCP listener, sends tool calls for an `ALLOW` tool with fresh request ids, or events, as fast as it likes: the database pool is exhausted for every tenant, the bound account's API quota at the system is burnt, `tool_call` and the audit chain grow without bound, and a member's personal host can write about 20 GiB of events per run into the shared PostgreSQL and replay them to SSE viewers. Threat-model 5.12 says "a flood of them" is mitigated; it is not.
- **Fix:** a per-run semaphore on `TOOL` requests (beyond it, `REJECTED TOO_MANY_REQUESTS`), a per-host and per-company bucket on `run.request` and `run.event`, the 256 KiB payload and 32 KiB text-chunk caps enforced server-side, a per-run byte budget, and the same cap in the host's listener.

### M5. The runner host's shared listener and uploads can be exhausted by one agent

- **Where:** `runner/crates/tinfirm-runner-host/src/git_proxy.rs:62-70,886-935` (64 connections for every run on the host, header timeout only, the permit held for the connection's life); `mcp.rs:2075-2078` (body read with no deadline); `mcp.rs:62,1791-1822` and `attachments.rs:355-389`, `host.rs:1728-1750` (`save_artifact` reads up to 100 MiB into memory, scans and copies it into multipart, with no concurrency cap; the server refuses above 25 MB only afterwards).
- **What:** an injected agent that opens 64 slow connections to its own listener stops git fetch, `ask`, `finish`, `save_artifact` and every server tool for every other run on the host until its own run ends; 30 to 64 parallel uploads of a 100 MiB file take the host to several GiB and an OOM kill, which loses every run (R36). Without `--egress-proxy` or `--sandbox-bridge` any container on the bridge can do the first without a secret.
- **Fix:** a whole-request deadline, an idle timeout, per-peer and per-route caps (about 8); `stat` and refuse above the server's limit before reading, a host-wide upload semaphore, streamed multipart.

### M6. Parked `ALLOW` tool calls run while the company's runs are stopped or the trigger is paused

- **Where:** `conversation/internal/ParkedCalls.java:134-205` (`bound`), `:270-330` (`take`): only `agent.paused()` is checked. Compare `ApprovedCalls.java:174`, which checks `runsStopped`. `run/internal/RunSwitches.java:52-56`: the stop cancels running runs only. `AccountService:268` refuses a connect only for `SYSTEM_PAUSED`.
- **What:** an agent's `ALLOW` write calls that wait on a Connect or `RECONNECT_*` are parked. Admins who notice a runaway or injected agent stop the company's runs and pause the trigger; when the credential is signed in again or the person connects, the parked writes execute, outside any run, with the kill switch on.
- **Fix:** in `bound` and `take`, leave `ALLOW` calls parked (or refuse them) while runs are stopped or the trigger is paused; add the paused-trigger check to `ApprovedCalls.prepare`; have the company's stop cancel parked calls.
- **Docs:** package-6 rule G4 is applied to approved calls and not to parked ones.

### M7. A member demoted to VIEWER keeps their triggers, service accounts and spend

- **Where:** `principal/internal/PrincipalsImpl.java:79-102` (`mayUse`, `usableServiceIdentities`: no role check, a VIEWER in a group gets the group's service accounts); `trigger/internal/TriggerGateImpl.java:187-268` (`entitled`: no role check); `TriggerService.java:1421-1423` (reassign accepts a VIEWER); `MemberRoleChanged` is consumed only by `conversation/internal/DecisionRouting.java`.
- **What:** "VIEWER never authors a trigger" and "a trigger's author always holds a seat" (ADR-0077 decision 5) are enforced only by the VIEWER's own session guard. A demotion by an admin, by the identity provider's role claim (ADR-0088) or by the "make Members Viewers" renewal leaves every trigger running with the member's accounts, budget and their groups' service accounts; reassigning to a VIEWER is accepted. Seats are defeated for automation.
- **Fix:** exclude VIEWER in `usableServiceIdentities`; pause `NOT_ENTITLED` in the gate when the author is a VIEWER; a `MemberRoleChanged` listener in the trigger module that runs the gate; refuse VIEWER as a reassign target and as `forPrincipalId`.

### M8. MCP OAuth discovery accepts endpoints on any origin, and re-discovery moves stored refresh tokens

- **Where:** `system/internal/oauth/OAuthDiscovery.java:184-217` (`usable`: issuer equality and S256 only; each endpoint passes `http.urlProblem()` and nothing relates it to the issuer); `SystemService.java:612-659,756-795` (`setApp` re-runs discovery and replaces the metadata; accounts untouched); `SystemCallsImpl.exchange/refresh/revoke` send to the stored endpoints.
- **What:** (a) a company MCP server whose metadata names a real provider's `authorization_endpoint` and its own `token_endpoint`, with the admin's own client at that provider and Tinfirm's callback registered: a member who clicks Connect sees the real consent page, and Tinfirm posts the code and PKCE verifier to the attacker, who redeems them at the provider for a token on the member's account there, wider than "a token for that server". Needs an admin and a member's click. (b) `PUT .../app` with an unchanged `baseUrl` may change the token and revocation endpoints while every account stays; the next refresh or disconnect sends each member's refresh token to the new endpoint. A vendor's server that turns hostile, plus one "set up again" by an admin, hands the tokens over.
- **Fix:** require the four endpoints on the issuer's origin (or a short explicit allow-list for split-hosted providers); on `setApp`, remove OAuth accounts and credentials when the token or revocation endpoint changes (ADR-0079's rule extended from the base URL); or pin the issuer and token endpoint on the account row.
- **Docs:** threat-model 8.7, 8.22 and CONVENTIONS §14.4 ("outbound requests go only to the system's origin") do not hold for these endpoints.

### M9. An MCP server's `tools/list` is unbounded in count, schema size and CPU

- **Where:** `system/internal/mcp/McpClient.java:109-132` (`ArrayList.contains` per tool, quadratic; 10 pages of 16 MiB); `system/internal/CatalogueService.java:297-352` (one INSERT per tool in one transaction); `V001__baseline.sql:937-958` (`input_schema jsonb` unbounded, no row cap).
- **What:** a member's personal MCP server (on by default) returning 16 MiB of tiny unique tools per page costs 10 to 20 minutes of a core on a request thread per page (measured: 50 000 tools 4.4 s, 100 000 tools 20 s), hundreds of MB of transient heap per page, and if it completes, a `system_tool` table with schemas of up to 16 MiB each that every run's tool list then carries.
- **Fix:** cap the catalogue (a few hundred tools, 64 KiB per schema, 1 to 2 MiB in all) and fail the refresh with the reason; a `HashSet` for names; 1 MiB per page; a refresh rate limit per system.

### M10. The Markdown renderer lets an attribute-bearing tag through, and the Outlook reply form takes it unsanitised

Reproduced by the reviewer with marked 18.0.14 and Angular 22.2.3's sanitizer.

- **Where:** `web/src/app/shared/markdown.ts:100-104` (the `text` renderer trusts marked's `escaped` flag: `'escaped' in token && token.escaped ? token.text : escapeHtml(token.text)`); `web/src/app/pages/outlook/task-pane.ts:852` (`displayReplyForm({ htmlBody: renderMarkdown(markdown) })`, no sanitizer, no CSP); `identity/internal/OutlookAddInProperties.java:34-45` (Outlook-page CSP allows whole script hosts, `ajax.aspnetcdn.com` included).
- **What:** marked marks text after an inline `<kbd>`, `<code>`, `<pre>` or `<script>` open tag as escaped, and such text can begin an unterminated tag that the following `</p>` closes. Angular's sanitizer strips scripts and handlers but keeps `class`, `href`, `src` and `target`, so `<a class="fixed inset-0 z-50 ..." href="https://attacker/...">` from a comment, chat message, agent output, tool result, memory note or issue becomes a full-viewport overlay on the authenticated page (a click on Allow goes to the attacker), and `<img src="/api/...">` makes same-origin GETs. In the Outlook pane, "Reply with this" puts the same HTML into the draft without the sanitizer or the CSP: an email-steered agent can plant a remote image that exfiltrates when the draft renders or is sent.
- **Fix:** always `escapeHtml(token.text)` in the `text` renderer and add specs for `<kbd> <a ...` and `<code> <img ...`; build the Outlook reply from escaped text or an allow-list without `img` and `class`; path-scope the Outlook CSP to `https://appsforoffice.microsoft.com/lib/1/hosted/` and the exact `MicrosoftAjax.js` URL.
- **Docs:** contradicts threat-model 1.25 ("shows raw HTML as text"). The CSP item extends R50.

### M13. Release and rolling assets are verified only by a checksum beside them, and installed with Docker-group power

- **Where:** `deploy/release/assets.sh` (`cmd_publish` with `--clobber` and a moved tag); `deploy/compose/auto-deploy/tinfirm-auto-deploy.sh:~296-330` (`check_sum` against the same release's `SHA256SUMS`), `:~703-771` (image accepted when its revision label equals the attacker-controllable `REVISION`); `tinfirm-auto-deploy.service` (a user in group `docker`, no sandboxing); `runner/README.md:62-76` (`sha256sum -c`); ADR-0081 point 7.
- **What:** whoever writes the `rolling` assets (H3) controls the checksums too. An auto-deploy host copies the tarball's `compose.yaml` over the live one, runs `docker compose up` (a service with `privileged: true` or a host mount is root and reads `.env` with the vault keys), installs the runner host binary and reloads the unit, within five minutes. Customers cannot distinguish a replaced release binary with the published procedure; image signing covers images and chart only.
- **Fix:** sign `SHA256SUMS` (or each asset) with the cosign key in the `release` job and verify the signature in `tinfirm-auto-deploy` and the Ansible role; sign `rolling` with a CI-held key pull-request workflows cannot read, or have the host pull a digest recorded in a deployment repository; `NoNewPrivileges`, `ProtectSystem`, `PrivateTmp` on the unit.

### M14. Nothing asserts that the runtime database role cannot bypass RLS, and no test guards "every tenant table is forced"

- **Where:** absent. Only `deploy/compose/postgres/init.sql` creates the roles `NOSUPERUSER NOBYPASSRLS`; the Helm path leaves them to the operator (threat-model 2.5 "role attributes: Operator"). `RowLevelSecurityTest` checks the tables it names.
- **What:** ADR-0009 rests on `tinfirm_app` being a plain role that owns nothing. An external database where it was created with `BYPASSRLS`, as a member of `rds_superuser`/`cloudsqlsuperuser`, or where `SPRING_DATASOURCE_USERNAME` names a member of `tinfirm_owner`, voids every policy silently, and nothing in the server, the probes or CI notices. A future migration that forgets `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` on a new `company_id` table passes CI. This is not an attack by itself; it turns any later `WHERE company_id` bug from fail-closed into cross-tenant read.
- **Fix:** a startup assertion (`rolsuper` and `rolbypassrls` false for `current_user`, no owned relations, not a member of `tinfirm_owner`; refuse to start otherwise), and a CI schema test that every table with `company_id` has forced RLS and a policy, with an explicit allow-list for the 18 instance-level tables.

### M15. Repository mirrors are shared by URL, and a workspace clone copies their unreachable objects

- **Where:** `runner/crates/tinfirm-runner-host/src/mirror.rs:52-61` (key = SHA-256 of the URL), `:294-325`, `:356` (`gc.auto=0`), `:483-516` (prune only in maintenance); `git.rs:288-318` (`git clone --no-hardlinks --no-checkout` from the mirror path).
- **What:** every run on the host that names the same repository URL shares one bare object store, and a local clone copies the whole `objects/` directory, reachable or not. Objects one conversation fetched with a more privileged account (a branch since deleted, history since rewritten, a secret since removed) are handed to the next run of another member whose account could not fetch them, readable by `git cat-file`. A company host serves one company and a personal host one owner (ADR-0092), so the disclosure is between members and conversations of one company, not across companies. The clone behaviour is git's documented local-clone semantics; not exercised.
- **Fix:** key mirrors by the account or credential that fetches (or at least by principal), or clone with a reachability-limited transfer (a bundle of the run's refs, or `git gc --prune=now` under the mirror lock before each clone).

### M16. The SCIM first-sign-in binding excludes OWNERs but not instance admins

- **Where:** `identity/internal/ExternalProvisioning.java:471-473` (the filter: `authSource == SCIM`, active, `!ownsACompany`); `UserAdminService.java:106` (instance admin can be granted to any user, a SCIM user who has not signed in yet included).
- **What:** the rule "never an OWNER, so that a SCIM token cannot point an owner's account at a subject its holder controls" does not cover `instanceAdmin`, a higher privilege that crosses companies. Once an instance admin grants it to a SCIM-provisioned person before their first sign-in, the company's SCIM and IdP decide which identity becomes an instance admin. No test covers it.
- **Fix:** add `!user.isInstanceAdmin()` to the binding filter, or refuse `grantInstanceAdmin` while `authSource == SCIM`, with a test. Fix together with H1.

### M17. Session filters pass through principals that are not Tinfirm users; a non-OIDC registration bypasses provisioning

Confidence Low to Medium: traced in code, not run; it needs an operator-configured registration without the `openid` scope.

- **Where:** `identity/internal/ActiveUserFilter.java:47` and `AuthorizationServerUserFilter.java:47` (any other authenticated principal passes unchanged); `SecurityConfiguration.java:133-142` (only `oidcUserService` is customised; nothing requires `openid`); `OAuthRequestValidators.java:68`, `OAuthCompanyAccess`, `OAuthAccessTokenCustomizer` (treat `principal.getName()` as a user id).
- **What:** a registration whose scopes lack `openid` takes Spring's plain OAuth2 path: `DefaultOAuth2UserService`, no `ExternalProvisioning` (no `(issuer, subject)`, no deactivation check), an `OAuth2AuthenticationToken` in the session, which the two filters keep. On the web API such a session gets 401 from `CurrentActor`; on the authorization server's chain the IdP-supplied name is used as a Tinfirm user id, so a name equal to a member's UUID would be consented and tokens minted as that member.
- **Fix:** fail startup for any registration without `openid` in its scopes; in both filters invalidate the session when the principal is not a `SessionUser`.

### M18. The reusable workflows pin every action by a mutable tag

- **Where:** `.github/workflows/reusable-release.yml`, `reusable-security.yml` and `reusable-hotfix-branch.yml` (the release, the security scan, the hotfix branch) and the composite actions under `.github/actions/` that publish the chart and log in to the registry.
- **What:** the reusable workflows and composite actions name every third-party action they `uses:` by a tag, which resolves at run time. One of those tags receives the release App's private key (`actions/create-github-app-token@v3`, in the release's `tag` and `next`), one receives the GHCR token and builds the release images (`docker/build-push-action@v7`), and `actions/checkout@v6` runs with the release App's token persisted. A compromised major tag of any of them (the 2025 `tj-actions/changed-files` incident is this class) runs in every release and every push to `main`, with those secrets. The security workflow turns off the semgrep rule that would have flagged this. The scanner images are pinned by version tag, not digest (`semgrep/semgrep:1.173.0`, `zricethezav/gitleaks:v8.30.1`, `aquasec/trivy:0.74.0`).
- **Fix:** pin every third-party action in the reusable workflows and composite actions by commit SHA with the tag in a comment, as the other workflows do, and let Dependabot's `github-actions` entry move them; pin the scanner images by digest; drop the semgrep exclusion once the pins are in.
- **Docs:** threat-model 6.3 ("Built: every action pinned by SHA") does not hold for the third-party actions of the reusable workflows and composite actions.

Egress proxy: per-run listener and secret, exact and suffix rules, every resolved address vetted (loopback, link-local, multicast, reserved, metadata literals, NAT64 and 6to4-embedded, private unless allowed, the bind and local addresses), `Host` rewritten to the vetted authority, hop-by-hop and `Proxy-Authorization` stripped, a ready VM's slot refusing until bound. Credential files 0600 in 0700 directories, symlinks refused; rustls with system roots and no `--insecure`; no token, prompt or URL secret in logs; maintenance deletes only directories it enumerates. Bridge: TLS 1.3 with a pinned certificate, a one-time ticket per run and epoch, frames bounded; VM driver: firewall alone on the sandbox tag, `delete` refusing `k8s*` droplets, user data with a one-time ticket and no run credentials.
- **Web app.** No `bypassSecurityTrust`, `DomSanitizer`, `document.write`, `eval`, `srcdoc`, `postMessage`, `window.opener`; the one `[innerHTML]` goes through Angular's sanitizer (M10 is the renderer in front of it); links and images allow-listed, external images become links, own-file references resolve only to same-conversation URLs; downloads are `Content-Disposition: attachment` with `nosniff` and `no-store`, HTML previews shown as source; `safeReturnUrl` and the consent and link pages are as documented (same-origin paths, POST consent with a constant-time CSRF check, unframeable, identity named before linking); strict CSP (`script-src 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `object-src 'none'`, no `unsafe-eval`), `X-Frame-Options`, HSTS; CSRF on every mutating request including login and logout; no CORS; idempotency keys from `crypto.randomUUID()`; static serving without traversal or SPA fallback for API prefixes.

No secret in this repository (scanned for every common token format and PEM keys; only documented test constants).
- **Domain authorization.** `AccountsImpl.check` runs at every use through `BindingService.use` (system present and not paused, personal servers on, member and holder ACTIVE, grant and group membership, use right and lending, credential OK, enabled, not a bot, same identity) and never re-binds except ADR-0072's follow; `ToolRules` applies the three layers in order and settings only narrow; an `ALLOW` on a read becomes `ASK` when the tool writes; a READ grant makes the workspace read-only and the outbox drops writes; widening is pinned by version; the `Decider` is `NONE` for a VIEWER or a suspended member, the OWNER gets `CONVERSATION_BOUND` on others' decisions, scope `AGENT` needs the edit right; conversations, triggers and spend are visible as documented with 404 otherwise and the OWNER's reads audited; the VIEWER write guard covers every session write and `ToolInvoker` refuses VIEWER writes; trigger rows and payer are checked for the author at save and the gate runs before every firing with listeners for suspension, use-right, group, share, credential and reconnect changes; Q35 holds; departures and suspensions cascade; `RUNS_STOPPED` is honoured by dispatch, approved calls, approvals, firings, polls and the outbox (M6 is the parked-call gap); memory notes and compaction output are scanned, a trigger's notes are `PROPOSED` until reviewed, compaction uses the company's key; a run downloads only its assigned skill versions from its own host and epoch; the MCP endpoint checks scopes in the filter and again in `ToolInvoker`, an instance admin who is not a member gets 404; submission keys are per principal and a reuse with another body is 422.

## 8. Design pass: the design itself, challenged

Added 2026-10-27 after sections 1 to 7. Those sections asked whether the code does what the design says. This one asks whether the design is the right one: it takes the positions an adversary can hold, asks what each is handed by intent, and judges the accepted risks and the threat model's coverage from there. Nothing was executed. Findings new to this section are D1 to D9; each is a design defect (a control the design lacks, not a coding error), written as the file and rule it concerns, who it affects, the consequence, and the fix. Section 8.4 re-examines the accepted risks; 8.5 lists what the threat model leaves out; 8.6 argues the architectural choices.

### 8.1 Positions and what the design hands each on purpose

| Position | What it is given by design | Where the design holds |
|---|---|---|
| Anonymous | The login page, the OAuth authorization server, the SAML ACS, and the webhook, chat, SCIM and runner endpoints behind their secrets | Holds: every pre-authentication path checks a signature or a hash before parsing; nothing beyond denial of service (L2, L6, L11, L24) was found. The strongest part of the platform |
| A MEMBER | Agents, triggers, their own accounts, a personal MCP server, a personal runner host (a machine the member controls that the server treats as a runner for their own work), their budget, service accounts through groups | Holds for data: a member reaches only their own conversations and accounts. Weak for influence: a member shapes what other people's agents do (D1) and what company credentials are exposed to (D2) |
| An ADMIN | Every system, credential, lending, agent and trigger; kill switches; the audit | Weak: an admin acts with any member's own accounts (H2) and the design documents it |
| A company on a shared instance | Its own SCIM token, identity provider and admins | Weak: the instance-wide user table, SCIM adoption by email, SAML entity IDs, PATs and add-by-email cross the tenant line (H1, H4, M16, L5, D4) |
| Whoever writes where a trigger listens | Driving the agent with the trigger's accounts and budget (R41) | Holds as accepted for the author's own accounts; not for a lent WRITE credential (D2) |
| A third-party system (an MCP server, a tracker) | Tool descriptions, tool results, and every argument the agent passes to its tools | Weak: a READ tool receives arguments, so any system is a data sink for everything else the conversation read (D3) |
| Code in the sandbox | Execution in the container; the platform listener; the egress allowlist; the git proxy | Holds: no escape, no credential; the provider tunnel (M1) and the host's shared listener (M5) are the gaps |
| A runner host | Every run assigned to it: prompts, workspace git tokens, one-run model tokens | Holds for personal hosts (only the owner's work; M2 is a timing gap); a company host is a second server in everything but hardening (D7) |
| Write access to a branch or the CI | Workflows that run with the repository's secrets, on the runners that sign the release (H3, M13, M18) | Weak until branch workflows can no longer reach the deployment keys or the signing jobs |

### 8.2 Design findings

**D1. Edits to a shared agent reach everyone without acceptance, so the agent's owner steers other people's conversations.** Severity High (design).
- Rule: PLATFORM §6 "widening is accepted, narrowing is not; instructions and descriptions are not widening"; threat-model 9.11 accepts the same for skills. `agent/internal/AgentVersions.java:72-98` classifies only the ceiling (systems, tools, modes, repositories, hosts); `AgentCallers.java:85-93`: the owner and admins edit.
- Who: everyone who uses a shared agent with their own accounts, the OWNER included, against the agent's owner (any MEMBER) or an admin.
- Consequence: a person accepts an agent's ceiling once, say a GitHub repository at WRITE, a network host, an `ALLOW` write tool. From then on, whoever edits the agent decides what it does with that person's accounts inside that ceiling, with no acceptance, no notice and no version the person can compare. The ceiling is the limit of the damage; the instructions are the damage. An agent shared with many members is the highest-leverage object in the company, and the design treats its text as harmless.
- Fix: version the instructions and skills with the ceiling; when the current ceiling has any `ALLOW` write tool, WRITE repository or network host, treat an instruction change as widening (acceptance per person and per trigger author, a diff on the acceptance page); otherwise at least audit `agent.instructions_changed`, show "changed by X on date" in the conversation, and let a person pin the version they reviewed. Add an accepted-risk row for whatever remains.

**D2. A trigger open to anyone needs no acknowledgement when it writes with a lent credential, and the admin who lent it is never told.** Severity High (design).
- Rule: PLATFORM §7.2 and `trigger/internal/TriggerEditor.java:393-404`: `needed = writes.stream().anyMatch(OpenWrite::own)`; the refusal text recommends "use a service identity's account" as the way out. R41's reason: "a READ lending bounds what such a trigger can do".
- Who: the company, against any MEMBER with the use right on a service account that borrows a WRITE credential (R43), on a Jira, GitHub, GitLab, chat or webhook source anyone can write to (a Service Management portal, a public repository's issues, a channel with guests).
- Consequence: the member exposes a company credential's write access to everyone who can write at the source, without acknowledging anything, and the admin who lent the credential has no page that shows which open triggers use it. The acknowledgement protects the member's own account and nothing else; the "safe" option the editor offers is the one that moves the exposure onto the company.
- Fix: require the acknowledgement for every open write, own or lent; let a lending or grant carry "may be used on triggers open to anyone" (default no), decided by whoever lends; list open triggers on the credential's and the service account's pages; tell the lender when a trigger exposes their credential.

**D3. A READ tool is a data sink: the effect axis (READ, WRITE) does not capture "sends data to the system".** Severity Medium (design).
- Rule: PLATFORM §10, the three layers; `ToolRules` (verified in §5). A tool's arguments go to the system verbatim.
- Who: the company, against any third-party system an agent may call (a vendor's MCP server, a personal server, a tracker), when the same conversation also reads code, issues, documents or memory.
- Consequence: everything a conversation reads can be sent to any system it may call, as the arguments of a READ tool, with no approval, because READ is `ALLOW` by default. The design's data-flow control is the sandbox egress allowlist; server-executed tools bypass it by construction. Prompt injection (R41) is the trigger, but the design is what makes the flow legal.
- Fix: a trust class per system ("may receive data from other systems": yes for the company's own trackers and code hosts, no by default for MCP servers), enforced as `ASK` on the first call to a lower-class system after a higher-class read in the conversation; argument size caps; the class shown on the system's page. If not built, an accepted-risk row that says so in these words.

**D4. Companies on one instance are not an actor in the design, so several instance-wide objects cross the tenant line.** Severity High (design; the symptoms are H1, H4, M16, L5).
- Rule: ADR-0075 (companies from the deployment, one user table); CONVENTIONS §6 (an OWNER or ADMIN adds a member by email); `company/internal/MemberService.java:129-130` (`USER_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown email, otherwise any instance user is attached, unilaterally); `V001__baseline.sql:371-383` (`api_token` has no company column: a PAT acts at every company of the user); `scim_company_id` stored and unused (H1); the SAML entity ID (H4); `MemberRoleChanged` and departures per company but the user instance-wide.
- Who: every company on an instance that hosts companies which do not trust each other's admins (the Enterprise case), against the admins of any other company.
- Consequence: besides H1 and H4, an admin of company A learns whether any email has an account on the instance (R3's reason, "only company admins see it", assumes one company), can attach any instance user to A without the user's consent (which makes that user's personal runner host present in A and lets A's admins reassign work to them, H2), and a PAT or a compromised session of a user in A carries that user's memberships in B. The threat model's rows are written per boundary with STRIDE letters and never name the tenant adversary, so each of these passed as "a company's own admin".
- Fix: a sentence in the threat model that says whether the companies of one instance trust each other's admins. If they do not: the H1, H4 and M16 checks; an invitation the user accepts instead of a unilateral add when the email already has an account; `USER_NOT_FOUND` replaced by a generic answer; a company list on PATs and a per-company grant on the OAuth consent (the grant already records its company); SCIM and SAML identity bound to the provider's company. If they do: say so, and the symptoms become accepted risks with a reason.

**D5. Kill switches are enforced at several points instead of one.** Severity Medium (design; M6 is the symptom).
- Rule: CONVENTIONS §15.13; `RunSwitches`, `ApprovedCalls`, `ParkedCalls`, the outbox claim, the pollers and the firings each consult `CompanySwitches` on their own; `AccountsImpl.check` (the credential reveal, verified in §5) checks the system's pause and the principals' state but not `RUNS_STOPPED` or a paused trigger.
- Consequence: every new path that uses a credential must remember every switch; one did not (M6). A company's stop is the control an admin reaches for during an incident and the one most likely to be bypassed by the path nobody thought of.
- Fix: make the reveal the gate: `BindingService.use` refuses, or waits, while the company's runs are stopped, the trigger is paused or the agent is paused, for every purpose but the outbox's own drops; then the other checks become early exits rather than the control.

**D6. The personal-host rule is enforced at dispatch, not at the reveal.** Severity Medium (design; M2 is the symptom).
- Rule: threat-model 5.14 "checked again under the dispatch lock"; `TurnSourceImpl.content` reveals tokens later, with `placement.hostKind()` unused.
- Fix: as D5, the reveal is the gate: a git token for a workspace of a run placed on a personal host is revealed only when the account's holder is the run's payer; otherwise the run goes back to the queue. One predicate at one point.

**D7. A company runner host is a second server in everything but its hardening.** Severity Medium (design; L27 and R27, R34, R44 are the symptoms).
- Rule: threat-model 5.13, 5.14, R27, R34; the Ansible role's defaults (L27); ADR-0007 "credentials stay in the deployment".
- Who: the company, against whoever compromises a company host (a VM that holds the Docker socket, the runner token, and during every run a git token and a prompt).
- Consequence: the design says credentials never enter the sandbox and keeps that promise, but a company host receives the git token of every conversation it runs, keeps it until the run ends after revocation (R44), and is installed by the documented role without the egress proxy, without the bridge, without systemd hardening and without a checksum on what it downloads. The threat model treats the host as semi-trusted and the operator guide treats it as a convenience VM.
- Fix: in the design, name the company host a tier-0 asset beside the server; in the product, short-lived per-run git credentials (the GitHub App of Q11 and A10; a push lease the server grants per run), `--egress-proxy` and `--sandbox-bridge` as the role's defaults, systemd sandboxing and required checksums (L27), and a host-side revocation of a run's git token when the server revokes it (R44).

**D8. The model relay treats the provider as a passive channel.** Severity Medium (design; M1 is the symptom).
- Rule: ADR-0059, threat-model 7.2 and R13 ("the model provider is still a channel"); `LiteLlmRequests` removes LiteLLM's fields and nothing else.
- Fix: in the design, classify request fields by what they make the provider do (call a URL, search the web, run code) as policy, off by default, with a company setting; in the relay, the allow-list of M1.

**D9. Bearer credentials for people's AI clients outlive the person's intent.** Severity Medium (design; L34 and D4 are the symptoms).
- Rule: ADR-0045; `api_token.expires_at` nullable (no default expiry), no company column; `PasswordService` ends sessions only; OAuth refresh tokens for 30 days.
- Fix: a default expiry on PATs (90 days) with renewal, a company list on each token, revocation of tokens and grants on a password change or reset and on departure from a company, and re-authentication to create one.

### 8.3 What the design gets right

The design pass is not only a list of gaps. These choices are correct and should be kept as they are: credentials never enter the sandbox and the server executes every tool (ADR-0007, ADR-0039); one account per system per conversation, fixed and re-checked at every use (§5.3); widening needs acceptance (§6); unconfirmed MCP tools count as WRITE; personal hosts run only their owner's work and company credentials never reach them (ADR-0092); the model proxy always on, with a one-run token and the key never leaving the server (ADR-0059); LiteLLM behind the server with a budget per principal; the vault's envelope encryption with row-bound AAD; the hash-chained audit with no personal data in hashed fields; tenancy by forced RLS with composite foreign keys; polling instead of inbound webhooks for trackers; companies and sign-in from the deployment rather than the app (ADR-0075); the small anonymous surface. Nothing in this section argues for replacing any of them.

### 8.5 What the threat model leaves out

- **Actors.** The rows carry STRIDE letters and name a boundary, never an adversary with a position. Missing as positions: a company on a shared instance (D4); the owner or editor of a shared agent (D1); a trigger's author as the person who decides what a company credential is exposed to (D2); a third-party system as a destination of data, not only a source of injection (D3); the company runner host as a target in its own right (D7); the CI as a path to every customer (H3, M13, M18). Writing the model per position, with what each is handed by design, would have produced most of this report's High findings from the design alone.
- **Time.** Lifetimes are an asset dimension the model does not have: PATs without expiry, 30-day refresh tokens, a git token for the length of a run after revocation, a rotated webhook secret kept 24 hours, a one-hour Teams or Google token replay window. One table of every credential with its lifetime and its revocation path would show which ones have none.
- **The reveal as the invariant.** The design states its rules per feature; the code enforces them per path. The one place every rule can be enforced at once is where a secret leaves the vault for a use (`BindingService.use`). Naming that point as the invariant, and listing what it must check (principal, holder, grant, lending, credential, system, company stop, trigger, agent, host kind, purpose), is the design change that would have prevented M2 and M6 and will prevent their successors.

### 8.6 Architectural choices, argued

1. **One long-lived LiteLLM key per principal (Q32).** Keep. The key never leaves the server; a per-run key would protect only against a copy of the server's database with its vault key, which is already total loss. The relay's one-run token is the right boundary.
2. **Server-executed tools as the single trust root (ADR-0039).** Keep; it is why no credential was found in a sandbox. Its cost is D3: the server is now the deputy for every cross-system data flow, and the design needs a data-flow axis beside the effect axis.
3. **Companies declared by the deployment, one instance-wide user table (ADR-0075).** Right for one company per instance. For several, the design must say whether their admins trust each other; if not, D4's list. The alternative, one instance per company, is what the Enterprise air-gapped bundle (§14) would give and is the simpler security story.
4. **"Access follows the audience" (§2, principle 1).** Correct, and incomplete: the audience decides whose accounts act, but the agent's definition decides what they do, and that definition belongs to whoever wrote it. The principle needs a second clause, "the definition is accepted by the people it acts for", which D1 and D2 make concrete.
5. **Semi-trusted runner hosts with a predicate at dispatch.** Replace the predicate's position: enforce at the reveal (D6), and treat company hosts as tier-0 (D7). The elastic runners (ADR-0095 to ADR-0100) move in this direction by making hosts disposable; the git credential's lifetime has to follow.
6. **Kill switches as a set of checks.** Replace with one gate at the reveal (D5), and make the company's stop also cancel parked calls and hold the outbox, which it does today only in part.
7. **Widening acceptance by version.** Keep; it is the best idea in the authority model. Its exclusion of instructions is the gap (D1).

### 8.7 What to do with this section

The five changes that close most of 8.2: a tenant-trust statement with D4's checks (also H1, H4, M16); the reveal as the one gate (D5, D6, with M2 and M6); acknowledgement and visibility for lent credentials on open triggers (D2); instruction changes as widening when the ceiling can write or reach the network (D1); a system trust class or an explicit accepted risk for READ tools as sinks (D3). The rest (D7, D8, D9) are schedule items the design already points at.
