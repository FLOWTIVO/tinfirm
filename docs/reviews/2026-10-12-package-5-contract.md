# Package 5 contract: chats and the webhook

Status: decided on review (§11) · 2026-10-12 · Owner: J. Balcar

The contract for package 5 of the [platform redesign](2026-10-12-platform-redesign.md#part-9-how-it-is-built--decided): the shared and personal spaces outside the app. A direct message to the Tinfirm bot in Slack or Microsoft Teams is a personal conversation that acts as its person; a channel trigger answers mentions of the bot in its channels with the accounts and budget its author chose; a generic inbound webhook lets any other management software fire a trigger. The contract holds the tables, the invariants they keep, the rules as functions later code calls (verifying what arrives, who is served, linking chat users, the mention grammar, firing, the write-back to a thread), the API, the errors, the audit actions and the tests. It builds on packages 1 to 3 as built (CONVENTIONS §15.1 to §15.7) and on the [package 4 contract](2026-10-12-package-4-contract.md) as decided on review (its §11): §2.3 lists the decisions package 5 rests on. It fills what the [package 3 contract](2026-10-12-package-3-contract.md) reserved for it: the place `CHAT_THREAD` and the origin `DIRECT_MESSAGE`, `external_id` and `external_author` on messages, `decided_by_name`, answers from a chat thread, approvals as chat buttons, and the grammar of mentions. The schema starts fresh (Part 9); package 5's migrations are `V017` to `V019`.

**Revised after review** (2026-10-12). Package 4's decided §11 is applied throughout (§2.3, §5.7, §5.12, §5.14), and the one real choice it leaves, the bot's failure, is open point 21. The Slack and Teams facts are corrected: private channels' events, Slack disabling a failing subscription, what Teams' token does not prove, Teams buttons by `Action.Submit`, Teams files moved to later, the Standard Webhooks key. The security points are taken: the webhook verifies before it counts, the link page names the chat user by what cannot be chosen and both sides are told, the bot's account is guarded in grants and deletes, secret caches are read again on a refusal. §2.2 now lists the public API package 5 needs from the `conversation` and `system` modules instead of their internal classes. Each open point says whether the review agreed with it or what changed.

## 3. Tables

### 3.1 Chat and webhook systems

A system is one Slack workspace, one Teams tenant or one webhook sender; `external_system_chat_workspace_uq`, on `(company_id, kind, coalesce(settings ->> 'teamId', settings ->> 'tenantId'))` where one is set, keeps one system per workspace or tenant per company. What the admin registers and what Tinfirm keeps:

| Kind | `base_url` | `settings` (validated per kind) | In the vault: the system's managed secrets, written and never returned | In the vault: the bot's account (§3.2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SLACK` | `https://slack.com/api`, fixed | `teamId`, `teamName`, `appId`, `botUserId` (from the bot token's `auth.test`), `transport` `EVENTS_API` or `SOCKET_MODE` | `SLACK_SIGNING_SECRET` (Events API); `SLACK_APP_TOKEN` (Socket Mode: an `xapp-` token with `connections:write`) | The bot token (`xoxb-`); `external_id` the bot user id |
| `TEAMS` | `https://smba.trafficmanager.net`, fixed: replies go to an activity's `serviceUrl`, which must have this origin | `tenantId`, `appId` (the Azure Bot's Microsoft App ID), `appType` `SINGLE_TENANT` | None: activities are verified with the Bot Framework's published keys | The app's password (a client secret of the app id); `external_id` the app id |
| `WEBHOOK` | None | `scheme` `STANDARD` or `SECRET_HEADER`, `secretHeader` (default `X-Tinfirm-Secret`), `mapping` (§5.12), `maxPerMinute` (1 to the company's maximum; the company's default when not set, §5.12) | `WEBHOOK_SECRET`, generated by Tinfirm; `WEBHOOK_SECRET_PREVIOUS` for 24 hours after a rotation | None: a webhook has no accounts |

`teamId` is fixed once set, and `botUserId` is kept while the system has a bot: a token whose `auth.test` names another workspace, or another bot user (another app, or the app reinstalled as a new bot), is `400 BOT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. `DELETE …/bot` clears `botUserId`, so the next `PUT …/bot` sets it afresh. The `oauth_client_*` columns of a `SLACK` system (package 1's `PUT /systems/{id}/app`) hold the same Slack app's client, for the user accounts of `history` (open point 13); a `TEAMS` system has none in package 5.

### 3.2 The bot

`external_system.bot_account_id` (built, I9) names the bot: an account of a service identity on that system, kind `TOKEN`, its secret a `SERVICE_ACCOUNT` company secret. An admin picks the service identity (one per chat system is the advice: `svc-slack-bot`). The bot's account is used only by `ChatPlatform` to receive and post (C9): never bound, never granted (`Grants.create`), never in a trigger's table (`BOT_ACCOUNT`). It is set, replaced and removed only through `PUT` and `DELETE /systems/{id}/bot`: the service identity's own account routes refuse it (`409 BOT_ACCOUNT_IN_USE`), and so does the deletion of its service identity (`409 SERVICE_IDENTITY_IN_USE`, naming the system), so the bot never disappears without `system.bot_removed`.

### 3.3 `chat_user`

What Tinfirm knows of a chat user, for the audience check (§5.3) and to write to them: `company_id`, `system_id`, `external_id` (the Slack user id; on Teams the user's `29:` id, which every activity carries), `aad_object_id` (Teams: the user's Entra object id, `aadObjectId`, when the activity carries one; anonymous and some federated users have none; `NULL` on Slack), `team_id` (the user's workspace or tenant), `audience` (`MEMBER`, `GUEST`, `EXTERNAL`, `BOT`, `DELETED`), `display_name` (at most 200 characters), `dm_ref` (the direct message's channel; on Teams its conversation reference and `serviceUrl`), `checked_at`, `last_seen_at`, `notice_shown_at`, `link_offered_at`, `refusal_told_at`. Primary key `(system_id, external_id)`: every sender has a row, so a refusal is told once an hour (§5.3). On Teams a link names the `aad_object_id` (§5.4). Deleted 90 days after `last_seen_at`; goes with its system.

### 3.4 `chat_channel`

The channels the bot is in: `company_id`, `system_id`, `channel_id`, `team_id` (a Teams team), `name`, `kind` (`PUBLIC`, `PRIVATE`, `SHARED`: Slack Connect or a Teams shared channel), `bot_member`, `archived`, `updated_at`. Primary key `(system_id, channel_id)`. Kept from the platforms' events, public and private channels' alike (§5.2), and a refresh (`users.conversations`; Teams' `GET /v3/teams/{teamId}/conversations`).

### 3.5 `chat_link_request`

A sign-in link (security review, row 5): `token_hash` (the primary key: the SHA-256 of 32 random bytes; the token is never stored), `company_id`, `system_id`, `external_id`, `display_name` and `team_id` of the chat user it was sent to, `held_event_ids` (the direct messages it holds, at most 5), `created_at` (shown on the page, §5.4), `expires_at` (10 minutes), `consumed_at`, `consumed_by_user_id`, `outcome` (`LINKED`, `REFUSED`). `chat_link_request_by_token(hash)`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, returns the ids, as `runner_host_for_token` does.

### 3.6 `chat_preference`

`(company_id, principal_id, default_agent_id, updated_at)`, primary key `principal_id`: the agent a member's direct messages go to without a handle (§5.6). An agent the member no longer uses or that was deleted counts as none.

### 3.7 `chat_event`

What arrived from a chat platform, kept to act once and in order.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id`, `company_id`, `system_id`, `event_id` | Unique `(system_id, event_id)` (C3): Slack's `event_id`; for a Slack button, `{user}:{action_ts}`; on Teams `{conversation.id}:{activity id}`, since a Teams activity's id is unique only within its conversation (a press is such an activity too, §5.8) |
| `kind` | `MESSAGE` (a direct message), `MENTION` (in a channel), `ACTION` (a button), `LIFECYCLE` (install, uninstall, a channel or user changed) |
| `thread_key`, `platform_ts` | `{channelId}:{threadId}` and the platform's time (Slack's `ts`, Teams' `timestamp`): a thread's events are handled in this order |
| `retry_num` | Slack's `X-Slack-Retry-Num` or Socket Mode's `retry_attempt` of the first receipt |
| `payload` | The normalized event (user, team, channel, thread, text, files, action), cleared once handled |
| `status`, `reason` | `RECEIVED`, `AWAITING_LINK` (held for a sign-in link, §5.4), `AWAITING_AGENT` (a direct message held for the agent picker, §5.6), `HANDLED`, `IGNORED`, `REFUSED`, `FAILED`; the reason of §7 |
| `attempts`, `next_attempt_at`, `claimed_until`, `received_at`, `handled_at` | §5.2 |

Rows are deleted 24 hours after `received_at`; a held row is dropped earlier, when its link expires (10 minutes) or after an hour without an agent chosen, and the sender is told. `chat_event_due(now, limit)`, `SECURITY DEFINER`, returns the oldest due row of each thread.

### 3.8 `app_user` (changed)

`oidc_tenant_id` and `oidc_object_id`, set at each OIDC login from the `tid` and `oid` claims of the ID token itself (`getIdToken()`, not the merged claims), when the issuer is `https://login.microsoftonline.com/{tid}/v2.0` and the `tid` claim equals the issuer's tenant; `NULL` otherwise and for local users. ## 5. Rules

### 5.1 Registering a chat system and its bot

**Slack** takes events one of two ways; the admin chooses per system (open point 1):

| | Events API | Socket Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Public URL | Needed: Slack posts events and button presses to Tinfirm | None: the server opens a WebSocket to Slack (`apps.connections.open`), outbound only |
| Verification | The signing secret: an HMAC per request | The app-level token opens the connection; no signature per event |
| Acknowledgement | `200` within 3 seconds | `{envelope_id}` sent back within 3 seconds |
| Retries | Up to three (`X-Slack-Retry-Num`, `X-Slack-Retry-Reason`); deliveries that keep failing get the app's event subscriptions disabled (§5.2) | Redelivered with `retry_attempt`, `retry_reason` |
| Replicas | Any `api` replica | At most 10 connections per app; Tinfirm holds two per system on `worker` replicas, one per advisory lock |
| What a leak gives | The signing secret: forged events | The app-level token: a share of the events in Tinfirm's place (row 20, accepted) |

The admin creates the system (`POST /systems`, `kind SLACK`, `slack.transport`), installs the app from Tinfirm's manifest (`GET /systems/{id}/chat/manifest`), stores the signing secret or app-level token (`PUT …/chat/secrets`) before Slack verifies the request URL, and sets the bot (`PUT …/bot {principalId, token}`). The manifest asks for the bot scopes `app_mentions:read`, `chat:write`, `chat:write.customize`, `im:history`, `im:write`, `users:read`, `channels:read`, `groups:read`, `files:read`, `files:write`, `reactions:write`; the events `app_mention`, `message.im`, `member_joined_channel`, `member_left_channel`, the public channels' `channel_archive`, `channel_unarchive`, `channel_rename`, `channel_deleted`, `channel_left`, the private channels' `group_archive`, `group_unarchive`, `group_rename`, `group_deleted`, `group_left`, `channel_shared`, `channel_unshared`, `user_change`, `app_uninstalled`, `tokens_revoked`; interactivity; the App Home's messages tab; and, for `history` (open point 13), the user scopes `channels:history` and `groups:history` with Tinfirm's OAuth callback as the redirect URL, the app's client id and secret then stored on the system (`PUT /systems/{id}/app`). Not `message.channels`, nor `channels:history`, `groups:history` or `mpim:*` as bot scopes (C7). `auth.test` with the token gives `team_id`, which becomes the system's `teamId`, and the bot user id (§3.1, `BOT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`).

**Teams** has no outbound transport: the Bot Framework posts activities to `{TINFIRM_PUBLIC_URL}/api/chat/teams/{systemId}/messages`, so Tinfirm must be reachable from the internet. The admin creates a single-tenant Azure Bot with the Teams channel, registers the system (`kind TEAMS`, `teams: {tenantId, appId}`), sets the bot with the app's password, and uploads the app package from `GET …/chat/manifest` (the bot in the `personal` and `team` scopes, no resource-specific consent; `supportsFiles` stays off while Teams files are later, open point 12). Setting the bot proves the password: a `client_credentials` token from `https://login.microsoftonline.com/{tenantId}/oauth2/v2.0/token` for `https://api.botframework.com/.default`.

`POST /systems/{id}/test` also checks the bot (`auth.test`; a token request), the transport (a Socket Mode connection opens) and the stored secrets. A system's status is `ERROR` while its bot is refused, its connections fail, or its event subscription seems disabled (§5.2).

### 5.2 Receiving: verification, acknowledgement, idempotency, ordering, limits

The platforms' endpoints are outside `/api/companies` and the session chain, without cookies or CSRF, as the runner's are; each reads at most 1 MiB raw (`413`) and finds its system through `chat_system_by_id` (C2).

- **Slack** (`POST /api/chat/slack/{systemId}/events`, `…/interactions`): `X-Slack-Request-Timestamp` within 300 seconds of the server's clock either way; `X-Slack-Signature` equal, in constant time, to `v0=` and the hex HMAC-SHA256 of `v0:{timestamp}:{raw body}` under the signing secret. Then the body is parsed; `url_verification` answers its challenge. A failure is `401`, stores nothing and is counted per system. The secret is kept in memory per replica for 10 minutes; a signature that fails is checked once more against the secret revealed again (at most once per system per 10 seconds), so that a secret regenerated through another replica works at once, since Slack keeps no overlap, and forged requests cannot make every request reveal it.
- **Socket Mode** (`SlackSockets`): events arrive only over the server's connections, opened through `ChatCalls.connect`; each envelope is acknowledged once its event is stored; a `disconnect` envelope opens a new connection first. An app-level token that `apps.connections.open` refuses is revealed again once before the system turns `ERROR`.
- **Teams** (`TeamsVerifier`): the bearer JWT's signature against the keys of `https://login.botframework.com/v1/.well-known/openidconfiguration` (cached a day, read again for an unknown `kid`), `iss` `https://api.botframework.com`, `aud` the system's app id, `exp` and `nbf` with five minutes' skew, the key's endorsements naming `msteams`, the `serviceurl` claim (so named, lower case) equal to the activity's `serviceUrl`, whose origin is the system's base URL (threat model 11.9), and the activity's tenant (`channelData.tenant.id`) the system's. Teams waits about 15 seconds for the answer to an activity; nothing but the insert runs in the request, and Tinfirm uses no `invoke` (§5.8).
- **What Teams' verification does not give.** The Bot Framework's JWT authenticates the connector, not the activity: unlike Slack's signature it is not bound to the body, and it stays valid for about an hour. Whoever captures one between the TLS termination and the server can post any activity, under any `from.aadObjectId`, for that hour, as long as the `serviceurl` claim matches. For Teams, the TLS endpoint of Tinfirm's public URL is therefore inside the trust boundary: no plaintext hop in front of the server, which the operator's guide says. The dedupe and the `serviceurl` and tenant checks are the mitigations. A residual beside row 20 (threat model 11.1, 11.9).
- **The acknowledgement.** One short transaction inserts the `chat_event` (`ON CONFLICT (system_id, event_id) DO NOTHING`), and the endpoint answers `200` at once; nothing else runs in the request. A Slack retry of a stored event is answered `200` and does nothing (C3). An event of another workspace is answered `200` with `X-Slack-No-Retry: 1` and stored nowhere.
- **When the database fails** the answer is `503`. Slack retries such a delivery up to three times; when deliveries keep failing it disables the app's event subscriptions, emails the app's collaborators, and sends nothing more until someone re-enables them on the app's Event Subscriptions page. Nothing tells Tinfirm, so each replica remembers the systems it answered `503`, and such a system that has stored no event for `tinfirm.chat.silence-after` (`PT30M`) since then turns `ERROR`, with an inbox item for admins naming the re-enable step (the operator's guide shows it); its next stored event clears both. Socket Mode has no such failure (open point 1). The Bot Framework does not redeliver an activity answered `503`: the sender sees that their message failed and may send it again; Teams has no `X-Slack-No-Retry` and needs none.
- **Handling** (`ChatInbox`), on `worker` replicas every `tinfirm.chat.tick` (`PT1S`): `chat_event_due` gives each thread's oldest `RECEIVED` row, claimed `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, so one replica handles a thread at a time and in the platform's order (`platform_ts`), whatever order the events arrived in. Calls to the platform (`users.info`, the Teams roster, file downloads) run outside any transaction (CONVENTIONS §10), then one transaction does the conversation's work. A failure is tried again after 1, 2, 4… seconds, 5 times, then `FAILED`, and the bot tells the sender to try again.
- **Ignored**, `IGNORED`: messages of bots and apps (Slack's `bot_id`, subtype `bot_message`; Teams gives a bot no other bot's messages), the bot's own, edits and deletions (the turn took the text; the conversation keeps it), joins and other subtypes, and a channel message that does not mention the bot natively (C7).
- **Limits** (row 15, C15; open point 11) are company settings that admins change (`PUT /settings`), seeded from the instance's (`TINFIRM_CHAT_MESSAGES_PER_USER_MINUTE`, 20; `TINFIRM_CHAT_MESSAGES_PER_USER_DAY`, 200; `TINFIRM_CHAT_EVENTS_PER_SYSTEM_MINUTE`, 600), as package 4's decision 15 does for firings: messages a minute and a day per chat user, events a minute per system. The excess is `REFUSED` `RATE_LIMITED`, answered `200` so that Slack does not retry, told to the chat user once a minute, and logged. The body's size stays the operator's: it protects the server, not a company's budget.
- **Lifecycle events** keep `chat_channel`, `chat_user` and `trigger_channel.state` current: Slack's public and private channel events of §5.1, `member_joined_channel` and `member_left_channel` for the bot, and `user_change`; Teams' `conversationUpdate` (the bot added or removed, `channelCreated`, `channelRenamed`, `channelDeleted`, `teamDeleted`) and `installationUpdate`. `app_uninstalled` and `tokens_revoked` run `Accounts.refused` on the bot (§5.14).

### 5.3 Who is served

`ChatAudience.of(system, chatUser)` answers from `chat_user`, read again from the platform when unknown or older than an hour:

| Audience | Slack | Teams |
|---|---|---|
| `MEMBER`: served | `users.info`: `team_id` the system's; not `is_restricted`, `is_ultra_restricted`, `is_stranger`, `is_bot` or `deleted` | The roster (`GET {serviceUrl}/v3/conversations/{id}/members/{userId}`): `userRole` `user`, `tenantId` the system's, `aadObjectId` present |
| `GUEST` | `is_restricted` (multi-channel) or `is_ultra_restricted` (single-channel) | `userRole` `guest` |
| `EXTERNAL` | Slack Connect: `users.info`'s `team_id` another workspace's, `is_stranger`, or `users.info` refused. The event's `user_team` may hint at it, but `app_mention` does not reliably carry it, and the rule never rests on it | Another `tenantId`, `userRole` `anonymous`, or no `aadObjectId` and unknown to the roster, whatever `from.role` says (Teams does not fill it reliably) |
| `BOT`, `DELETED` | `is_bot`, `bot_id`; `deleted` | Teams gives a bot no other bot's messages; a sender without `aadObjectId` is `EXTERNAL` above |

Only `MEMBER` is served (C6): a refused message is not stored, and the refusal is told once an hour per chat user (`refusal_told_at`; an ephemeral message on Slack; nothing on Teams, which has no ephemeral messages in channels) and audited as `chat.refused`. Answers and presses follow the same rule (row 9). A channel's asker needs no link: the trigger's accounts act, and the audit records the linked member when there is one. A direct message and an approval need a link (§5.4).

### 5.4 Linking chat users to members

A link is an account of the member on the chat system: kind `LINK`, `external_id` the chat user's identity (Slack's user id; on Teams the `aadObjectId`, not the `29:` id `chat_user` is keyed by, so a Teams user without one is never linked), `external_login` the readable name, no secret; or, on Slack, the member's own `OAUTH` account for `history`, whose identity is the same (open point 13). One identity is one member per system (I2), and a member has one identity per system (I1).

**Automatically, on Teams only** (row 6), at a Teams user's first verified event without a link: the audience is `MEMBER`; the activity's `from.aadObjectId` is *O* and its `channelData.tenant.id` is *T*, the system's `tenantId`; exactly one user has `app_user.oidc_tenant_id = T` and `oidc_object_id = O` (§3.8), is a member of the system's company with an `ACTIVE` principal, and holds no account on the system; *O* is no one's yet. Then `ChatLinks.automatic` creates the link (`account.connected`, `how` `AUTOMATIC`). The claims that count are exactly the `tid` and `oid` of the ID token itself (`getIdToken()`, never the login's merged ID-token and userinfo claims) of a login through issuer `https://login.microsoftonline.com/{T}/v2.0`, matched with the activity's `aadObjectId` and tenant. Never the login's `sub` (pairwise per application in Entra ID), `email`, `preferred_username` or the UPN (changeable and reassignable), or `name`. A company whose provider is registered with Entra's v1.0 endpoint (issuer `https://sts.windows.net/{tid}/`) gets no automatic link: the admin guide says to register the v2.0 endpoint, and meanwhile its users link by the sign-in link. **On Slack nothing links automatically** (open point 2).

**Otherwise once, by a sign-in link** (row 5), `ChatLinks.offer(system, chatUser)`: an unlinked `MEMBER` who sends the bot a direct message or presses a button gets, in the direct message, a link `{TINFIRM_PUBLIC_URL}/link/{companyId}#{token}` (a `chat_link_request`: 32 random bytes, kept hashed, for that chat user, single use, 10 minutes), at most one per 10 minutes; their direct messages meanwhile wait `AWAITING_LINK` (at most 5) and are handled once linked, else dropped when the link expires. The token is in the fragment, which the browser never sends, so it reaches no access log; the web app keeps it across the sign-in and sends it in the body of `POST /chat-links/read` and `…/confirm`. The page, after the Tinfirm login, names what it links by what the chat user cannot choose, and warns: "Link the Slack user U04ABC123 of the workspace *Acme* (T02XYZ789), who asked for this link at 10:02, 3 minutes ago, to your account j.doe@acme.example? Its display name, *Jane Doe*, is one anyone can set. Confirm only if you just sent the Tinfirm bot a direct message yourself." with the Owner notice (row 18). It does not show the Slack email: that would need `users:read.email` for every user of the workspace, for a hint the user id and the time already give. `ChatLinks.confirm(user, token)`, in one transaction: the caller is a member of the company with an `ACTIVE` principal (else `404`); the request is unexpired (`410 CHAT_LINK_EXPIRED`) and unused (`409 CHAT_LINK_USED`); the chat user is still `MEMBER`; the chat user is no one's (`409 IDENTITY_IN_USE`); the caller holds no other identity there (`409 ALREADY_LINKED`). The request is consumed whatever the outcome; the link is audited as `account.connected` with `how` `SIGN_IN_LINK`.

**Both sides are told** of a link, however it was made: the bot writes "Linked to {member}" with the notice in the direct message, and the member's inbox holds for 7 days "Your account was linked to Slack user U04ABC123 of Acme at 10:05. Not you? Unlink", whose button removes it (`DELETE /me/accounts/{systemId}`). A member tricked into confirming a forwarded link (threat model Q34) learns of it in the app.

**By the member's own Slack sign-in**: a member who connects their Slack account for `history` (open point 13) signs in to Slack as that user through the app's OAuth, which proves the user is theirs; the `OAUTH` account is their link and replaces a `LINK` of the same identity; a sign-in as another Slack user than their link is `409 ALREADY_LINKED`.

- **Unlinking**: the member, with the built `DELETE /me/accounts/{systemId}`; an admin, for a compromised chat account (R45), with `DELETE /members/{userId}/accounts/{systemId}`, the member's account on a chat system, `LINK` or `OAUTH` (open point 16). The member's open direct-message conversations stay, readable in the app; a message in them from the chat user is refused until it is linked to the same member again (C5).
- **A member who leaves**: their accounts go, links included (I8); their personal conversations end `PERSON_LEFT` (built); the channel and webhook triggers they author pause `AUTHOR_LEFT`, and their conversations wait (§5.14).
- **Guests and other organisations** are never linked: neither path links a chat user that is not `MEMBER`. A deactivated chat user (`DELETED`) keeps its link until the member removes it or leaves, and is not served.

### 5.5 The mention grammar

**One bot per chat system, agents by text handle** (open point 6).

- **The bot** is addressed with the platform's own mention: Slack's `<@{botUserId}>` (the `app_mention` event), Teams' `at` mention entity of the bot. The mention is taken out of the stored text. In a direct message no mention is needed.
- **An agent** is named by `@handle` written as text, read by ADR-0034's parser (`Mentions`, §2.2): `@` at the start or after a character that is not a letter, digit, `_`, `-`, `/`, `.` or `@`; the longest run of letters, digits and hyphens; matched ignoring case against the live agents' handles; nothing in code counts. The first handle that names an agent counts, the rest is text. A platform's own user mention (`<@U…>`) is never a handle.
- **In a channel**: `@Tinfirm question` goes to the channel's default trigger; `@Tinfirm @dev question` to the channel's trigger of `dev`; a handle without a trigger in the channel gets "No agent @dev answers here; ask @support or @analyst". **In a thread** with a conversation, its agent answers; another agent's handle gets "This thread is @support's; start a new message to ask @dev".
- **In a direct message**: `@handle` at the start of a message that starts a conversation names its agent (§5.6).

### 5.6 Direct messages: personal conversations

`DirectMessages.receive(event)`, after §5.2 and §5.3:

1. **The person**: the sender's link (§5.4); without one, the link offer and nothing else (C5). The member's principal is `ACTIVE`, and the bot is usable (§5.14).
2. **The thread** (open point 4). On Slack each top-level message starts a conversation, which the bot answers in that message's thread, and a reply in the thread continues it. Teams' personal chats have no threads: a message continues the member's open direct-message conversation on that system, unless it starts with another agent's handle or follows "New conversation" (a button on each answer), which ends the open one `SUPERSEDED` (`PlaceConversations.end`) and starts another; its `threadId` is the first activity's id.
3. **An existing conversation**: its person must be the sender's member now (else `NOT_YOURS`: "This conversation is another member's"); ended: "This conversation ended; send a new message to start one" (C8). Then `PlaceConversations.receive` with a `PERSON` message and its `external_id`: while questions are open the message answers them all (ADR-0033); else it is the next turn's, or waits during a run, as in the app.
4. **A new conversation**: the agent is the handle's, else the member's default (`chat_preference`), else a card of the agents they use (at most 10, the recently used first). The message then waits `AWAITING_AGENT` (at most 5 per chat user, for an hour, then dropped and the sender told); each button of the card carries the held event's id, and a press counts only from the same chat user. The press starts the conversation with that held message and may make the choice the default (open point 5). `Agents.access(…).uses` is checked (`"You may not use @dev"`). `PersonalConversations.start(companyId, personPrincipalId, agentId, message, CHAT_THREAD, DIRECT_MESSAGE, placeRef)` (§2.2) does exactly the app's start (CONVENTIONS §15.7): `Settings.firstUse`, the runtime and model access fixed, the refusals `MODEL_ACCESS_UNAVAILABLE` and `NO_HOST_FOR_CONVERSATION`, which the bot posts with their reason. The title is the message's first line.
5. **Files** (Slack only, §5.10) are downloaded outside any transaction, then added with `PlaceFiles.add` in the conversation's transaction, the starting one included, so the first turn has them.

Such a conversation is package 3's personal conversation in everything: the person's bindings (`Accounts.bind(Personal(…))`), budget or subscription, settings, tools and modes, prompt (`A conversation with {person} in a Slack direct message.`, the `chat` module's `PlacePrompts`), visibility. **Connect cards** link to the app: `BindingWaiting` on it queues a `CONNECT` card, "{agent} needs {system}: Connect in Tinfirm", whose button opens `/conversations/{id}?binding={systemId}`, where package 3's card connects by OAuth (returning there) or a token, or picks a grant; `BindingBound` updates it to "Connected, continuing". The first conversation of a chat user carries the notice (row 18): "This is a personal conversation: {agent} acts with your own accounts and budget. The Owner of {company} can read it for support and audit." (`NOTICE`, once, `chat_user.notice_shown_at`).

### 5.7 Channel triggers

A trigger with `source CHANNEL` on a chat system holds its channels (`trigger_channel`), the agent, the instruction, the settings, the account table and payer, the team, `results.review` and `max_firings_per_hour` (within the company's maximum): package 4's trigger. `TriggerEditor.check` adds, after its own steps and **for the author, whoever edits** (package 4, decision 7: an admin editing on the author's behalf passes the same checks, never their own): the system has a bot (`409 BOT_NOT_SET`); each channel is known, the bot is in it, it is neither archived nor shared (`400 CHANNEL_UNAVAILABLE`); the author is linked on the system and a member of each channel (`400 CHANNEL_NOT_JOINED`, open point 7); one trigger per agent per channel and one default (`409 CHANNEL_TAKEN`). A reassignment (`POST /triggers/{id}/reassign`, package 4 decision 6) runs the same checks for the new author. The editor picks channels from `GET /systems/{id}/chat/channels`, which for an admin editing on the author's behalf answers the author's channels (`authorPrincipalId`, §6), private ones included. A channel trigger is always open to anyone (package 4 §5.8): the preview names "any member of {workspace} in #support", and saving needs `acknowledgeOpenEvents` while an `ALLOW` write tool remains with the author's own account. An admin's save on the author's behalf needs it as well, is audited with the admin as the editor, and gives the author an inbox item.

`ChannelMentions.receive(event)`, after §5.2 and §5.3 (any `MEMBER`, linked or not):

1. **The thread**: `threadId` is the message's thread, or the message itself when it is top-level. A conversation on it: continue (step 4); ended: "This conversation ended; mention me in a new message" (C8).
2. **The trigger**: by the grammar (§5.5), among the channel's `ACTIVE` `trigger_channel` rows; none: a hint, nothing stored. A trigger the gate stops, or whose agent is paused: "{agent} is paused here" or "{agent} cannot answer now", nothing stored and nothing fired later (package 4, decision 10). The gate stops a trigger that is **paused**, by hand or by package 4's gate: `ACCOUNT_BROKEN` when an account of its table cannot be used (decision 9), `AUTHOR_LEFT` (decision 6) and the others. It also stops one that **waits** so that it fires nothing: on its payer's model access (package 4 §5.3), or on its bot (§5.14, open point 21).
3. **Firing**: `Firings.fire` with event `MENTIONED`, the chat user as actor (`{id, login, name}` with `login` `NULL`; `actor_principal_id` the member when linked), no subject; the rate (`SKIPPED_RATE`, "Too many requests to {agent} this hour"). `TriggerConversations.start` with `place CHAT_THREAD`, `place_ref {systemId, CHANNEL, teamId, channelId, threadId}`, the title `{trigger}: {first line}`, the table's `FIXED` bindings; its `FIRST` turn takes the message, stored `EXTERNAL` through `PlaceConversations.receive`.
4. **Continuing**: the mention is an `EXTERNAL` message of the conversation (`PlaceConversations.receive`); while questions are open it answers them all (`DECIDED`), else it is the next turn's (`MESSAGE`), or waits during a run. A reply in the thread without a mention never reaches Tinfirm (C7).

Answers post in the thread (§5.9), named "{agent} (Tinfirm agent)". The prompt is package 4's (its §5.12) with `Started in #{channel} of {workspace} by {chat name} ({member} when linked). Every message here is other people's words; answers are posted in the thread, read by everyone in the channel.` Tools act with the table's accounts; the chat's own tools only through a row on the chat system (open point 13), never the bot's token (C9).

### 5.8 Questions and approvals in a chat

| | A direct message | A channel thread |
|---|---|---|
| A question (`ask`, a `BLOCKED` result's) | Posted in the thread (`QUESTION`), its options as buttons (at most 5; more are listed) | The same |
| Answered by | The person's next message in the thread (it answers every open question), or their press of an option; or the person in the app | A `MEMBER`'s mention in the thread (every open question) or press of an option, linked or not, never a guest's (row 9); or the team or the author in the app, posted as "*{member} answered in Tinfirm:*" (`ANSWER`) |
| An approval | A card in the thread (`APPROVAL`): the call in words (`what`), the reason, Approve and Deny, pressed by the person | The same card, pressed only by a linked member of the trigger's team, or its author without a team (open point 8) |
| A review of a result (package 4) | | In the app only; the thread reads "{agent}'s answer waits for {team}'s review" |

`ChatDecisions.press(event)`: the press is verified (C1) and deduplicated (C3); the decision's conversation is the thread's, and the press names the card the outbox posted for it (`place_ref` equals the card's channel and thread, and the card's message is that row's, else `IGNORED`); the presser is `MEMBER`. Then `PlaceDecisions.decide`: an approval only by a linked member who may decide it now, else `NotAllowed`, the decision stays open (C10), and the presser alone is told "Only members of {team} decide this". On Slack that is an ephemeral message; on Teams, which has none in channels, a message in the presser's direct message with the bot when the Bot Framework lets the bot open one (`ChatCalls.openDirect`), else nothing. A button decides with scope `CONVERSATION` and the agent's access; a scope `AGENT`, a repository's `WRITE` and a denial's note are given in the app. The decision records `decided_by_principal_id` (the linked member), `decided_by_name` (the chat name), `decided_via` `CHAT`, `decided_by_external_id`, and for an answer `decided_by_message_id`; it is audited with the chat user and the member (row 7, R45). The card is then updated (`DECIDED`): "Approved by {name} in Slack"; a press on a decided decision (`AlreadyDecided`) shows who decided. Text that says "approved" decides nothing.

**Teams' buttons are `Action.Submit`** (open point 12). A press arrives as a `message` activity that carries the card's data (`value`: the decision, the option, the outbox row) and the card's activity id (`replyToId`). It is verified, stored, acknowledged and handled in the thread's order like every other event, and the card is updated through the outbox (`CHAT_UPDATE`, `PUT …/activities/{id}`), as on Slack. `Action.Execute` was not chosen: its `invoke` must be answered in the HTTP response itself, with the refreshed card or an error, within Teams' invoke deadline. That would put the presser's audience check (a roster call for a presser Tinfirm does not know), the decision and the turn it starts inside the request, on a second receiving path beside every other event's, against §5.2's rule that nothing but the insert runs there. What is lost is an answer only the presser sees, which Teams gives in channels to invokes alone: the refusal goes to their direct message instead.

### 5.9 Results back to the chat: the outbox

The `chat` module queues rows for every `CHAT_THREAD` conversation, personal or a trigger's, in the transaction that commits the cause; package 4's `OutboxDelivery` delivers them with writer `BOT` (C12).

| Cause | On | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| `RECEIVED` | A turn starts from a chat message | `CHAT_REACTION`: `eyes` on the message (Slack); a `typing` activity (Teams) |
| `RESULT` | `TurnRecorded` `SUCCEEDED`, after the review when the trigger asks | `CHAT_POST`: the turn's `AGENT` message (`PlaceConversations.result`: the summary, next steps, open questions, change requests and links); a `CHAT_FILE` per new document (Slack; a link on Teams) |
| `FAILURE` | `TurnRecorded` `FAILED` or `LOST` | `CHAT_POST`: "{agent} could not finish this ({failure class}). {The team {team} was told. \| Open it in Tinfirm.}" |
| `QUESTION`, `APPROVAL` | `DecisionRaised` (§5.8) | `CHAT_POST`, the card's content from `PlaceDecisions.find` |
| `ANSWER`, `DECIDED` | `DecisionClosed`: answered in the app; decided or cancelled | `CHAT_POST` of the answer; `CHAT_UPDATE` of the card |
| `CONNECT`, `CONNECTED`, `NOTICE` | §5.6; a rebind on resume (§5.14) | `CHAT_POST`, `CHAT_UPDATE` |

A turn that ends on its own questions posts only the questions (ADR-0036); a cancelled turn posts nothing. **Delivery**: per conversation in order, oldest first, through `ChatCalls` (§2.2). The bot's token comes from `ChatBot.token(systemId)`: `Accounts.reveal` with the bot's principal and purpose `chat-bot:{systemId}`, kept in memory per replica for 10 minutes. When the platform refuses it, it is revealed again once, so that a token set through another replica is used at once; only a second refusal counts. Teams' Bot Framework token is kept until it expires. Slack: `chat.postMessage` (`channel`, `thread_ts`, `username` "{agent} (Tinfirm agent)", the bot's own icon, `blocks`, `text`, `parse: none`, `link_names: false`, `unfurl_links: false`, `unfurl_media: false`, `metadata` naming the outbox row), `chat.update`, `reactions.add`, `files.getUploadURLExternal` and `files.completeUploadExternal` with `channel_id` and `thread_ts`. Teams: `POST {serviceUrl}/v3/conversations/{conversationId}/activities` with the thread's conversation id (`{channelId};messageid={threadId}` in a channel), `PUT …/activities/{activityId}` to update; a post shows as the bot and starts with **{agent} (Tinfirm agent)**. On both, the picture is the bot's own, never a person's (row 16; open point 20).

**Retry**: a `429` waits for its `Retry-After` (Slack allows about one post a second per channel; Teams limits per conversation and per bot); `tinfirm.chat.post-rate` paces one post a second per channel and 20 a second per system. `5xx` and no answer back off as package 4's rows do. `invalid_auth`, `token_revoked`, `account_inactive` or Teams' `401`, after the one reveal again, run `Accounts.refused` on the bot; the rows wait for a usable bot up to 7 days, then are dropped. `channel_not_found`, `not_in_channel`, `is_archived` drop the row and make the trigger channel `UNAVAILABLE`. An ambiguous attempt is tried once more and may post twice: neither platform takes an idempotency key, and without history scopes the thread cannot be read back (accepted). A delivered row records the message's `ts` or activity id as `external_id` and a `system_write` `CHAT_MESSAGE`, audited `source.written`. A dropped row posts a `SYSTEM` message in the app and reaches the person's, or the author's and team's, inbox.

### 5.10 Formatting, size and files

`ChatMarkdown.toSlack(markdown)` and `toTeams(markdown)`, new in the `chat` module, turn the agent's Markdown into each platform's text (open point 12):

| Markdown | Slack mrkdwn | Teams (`textFormat: markdown`) |
|---|---|---|
| `**b**`, `*i*`, `~~s~~` | `*b*`, `_i_`, `~s~` | As written |
| `[t](u)`, images | `<u\|t>` for http(s) only; an image as its link | Links; an image as its link |
| Headings | A bold line | A bold line |
| Lists, quotes, code, fences | `•` and numbered lines; `>`; as written, fences without their language | As written |
| Tables | A fenced block, columns aligned | A fenced block |
| `&`, `<`, `>` | `&amp;`, `&lt;`, `&gt;`, so `<!channel>`, `<!here>`, `<@U…>` and `<#C…>` stay text (C11) | `<at>` and HTML escaped |

- **Slack** posts Block Kit within Tinfirm's own limits, which sit inside Slack's (50 blocks a message, 3 000 characters a `section`): `section` blocks of mrkdwn of at most 3 000 characters, at most 40 per message, a `context` block ("{agent} (Tinfirm agent) · {key} · Open in Tinfirm"), an `actions` block for buttons, and a plain `text` fallback that Tinfirm cuts at 3 000 characters (Slack takes 40 000).
- **Teams** posts the body as a message, and cards (questions, approvals, Connect, notices) as Adaptive Cards 1.5 whose buttons are `Action.Submit` (§5.8), within about 28 KB per activity.
- **Splitting**: at most 12 000 characters a message on Slack and 20 000 on Teams, cut at a blank line, else a line break, never inside a fence (closed and reopened), parts marked `(1/3)`; more than 4 parts post the first and then the whole answer as `answer.md` (Slack) or "Read the full answer in Tinfirm" (Teams).
- **Files in**: a Slack message's files, downloaded from `url_private_download` with the bot's token (`ChatCalls.download`, through the outbound guard). Each passes package 3's limits (25 MB a file, 50 files, 250 MB) and the upload scan (`PlaceFiles.add`), and becomes the conversation's attachment noted "From Slack, {chat name}"; a refused file is left out with a note. **Teams files are not read in package 5** (open point 12): an attachment in a personal chat carries a pre-authenticated OneDrive or SharePoint `downloadUrl` (`https://{tenant}-my.sharepoint.com/…`), outside the Teams profile of the outbound guard, and a file in a channel is a SharePoint link that needs Microsoft Graph. The message is taken without it, and the bot answers "Files sent in Teams are not read yet; add the file to this conversation in Tinfirm: {link}".
- **Files out**: each new `DOCUMENT` output is uploaded into the Slack thread (at most 25 MB, read with `PlaceFiles.content`); on Teams it is a link to the output in Tinfirm.

### 5.11 Prompt injection in channels

Accepted (Part 4, R41): whoever may write in the channel drives the agent with the trigger's accounts. The mitigations, concretely:

1. **The editor warns** and needs the acknowledgement (§5.7); its one choice lists service identities first, and it fills the settings with `ASK` for every write tool.
2. **Only mentions enter**: the agent sees the thread's messages that mention the bot, never the channel's others (C7); the prompt marks every chat message and file, the asker's request included, as other people's words; `# Instruction` is the author's alone.
3. **Decisions** stay with the team: an asker approves nothing unless a linked member of it; typed words decide nothing (C10).
4. **The way out is closed**: the bot's posts cannot ping the channel or a person and Slack unfurls no link (C11), so an injected answer cannot make Slack fetch a URL that carries data; the sandbox reaches only the hosts the author accepted, and a `request` for another is the team's approval.
5. **Bounds**: `max_firings_per_hour` within the company's maximum, the per-user rates (C15), the payer's budget, one run at a time per conversation; guests, other organisations and shared channels are refused (C6, §5.7).

### 5.12 The generic webhook

**Registering** (admins): `POST /systems` with `kind WEBHOOK` and `webhook: {scheme, secretHeader?, mapping, maxPerMinute?}`. Tinfirm generates the secret (`whsec_` and 32 random bytes in base64) and answers it once with the URL `{TINFIRM_PUBLIC_URL}/api/hooks/{systemId}`; `POST …/webhook/rotate` makes a new one and accepts the previous for 24 hours. `maxPerMinute` is at most the company's maximum (`400 WEBHOOK_LIMIT_TOO_HIGH`), and the company's default when not given: company settings that admins change (`PUT /settings`, `webhookDeliveriesPerMinute: {default, max}`), seeded from `TINFIRM_WEBHOOK_DELIVERIES_PER_MINUTE_DEFAULT` (60) and `TINFIRM_WEBHOOK_DELIVERIES_PER_MINUTE_MAX` (600), as package 4's decision 15 does for firings. The system's page marks `SECRET_HEADER` as weaker and says what is lost (below).

**Receiving** (`WebhookInbox`, `api` replicas, `POST /api/hooks/{systemId}`), in this order, so that nobody without the secret spends the sender's rate (C1):

1. `application/json` (`415`), at most 1 MiB read raw (`413`).
2. A client address with more than 60 refused deliveries for this system in the last minute gets `429`, before anything else: the flood of a stranger who learned the URL, whose refusals are counted apart from the sender's deliveries.
3. `WebhookVerifier` (C1, row 14; open point 15); a failure is `401` without a body, stores nothing, and is counted for that client address.
4. Verified deliveries only: at most `maxPerMinute` a minute per system (`429` with `Retry-After`).
5. The body is parsed: at most 32 levels deep and 10 000 values (`400`).

The schemes:

- `STANDARD`: the headers `webhook-id` (1 to 200 printable characters), `webhook-timestamp` (Unix seconds) and `webhook-signature` (space-separated `v1,{base64}`); one listed signature equals, in constant time, the base64 HMAC-SHA256 of `{id}.{timestamp}.{raw body}` keyed with the decoded secret, the bytes of the base64 after `whsec_` (what a Standard Webhooks library signs with), or, for 24 hours after a rotation, the previous one; the timestamp lies within 300 seconds of the server's clock either way: row 14's five-minute window, and the libraries' own tolerance, so a sender whose clock runs a minute fast is not refused.
- `SECRET_HEADER`, for a sender that cannot sign: the header equals the secret in constant time; the delivery id is `webhook-id`, else the mapping's `deliveryId`, else the SHA-256 of the body. What is lost, which the system's page says: the secret travels in every request, so whoever captures one delivery can forge any until the secret is rotated; nothing bounds a replay's age, and a captured delivery is refused only while its id is remembered (7 days); without `webhook-id`, a replay with one byte changed is a new event. The page advises a rotation every 90 days and shows the secret's age; `rotate` keeps the previous secret 24 hours, while the sender is updated.

Then `webhook_delivery` is inserted `ON CONFLICT (system_id, delivery_id) DO NOTHING`: a known id is `200 {duplicate: true}` and nothing more; a new one is mapped and fired in the same transaction, `202 {deliveryId, firings}`.

**The mapping** (`WebhookMapping`): JSON Pointers (RFC 6901) only, no expressions: `event`, `subject.key`, `subject.title`, `subject.url`, `actor.id`, `actor.name`, `text`, `deliveryId`. A string, number or boolean is read as text and cut to its limit (key 200, title 500, URL 2000 and http(s), actor 200, text 20 000); anything else, or nothing, is `null`. A failure is `MAPPING_FAILED`, kept for the admins, and fires nothing. `POST …/webhook/map` tries a body without storing or firing.

**Triggers** (`source WEBHOOK`): fire on a delivery whose mapped `event` is one of their values (or any, without one) and whose conditions hold (`{pointer, op: EQUALS | IN | PRESENT | ABSENT, values}`, on the raw body). `TriggerEditor.check` as package 4's, without a source row, for the author whoever edits; always open to anyone who can make the sender call (threat model 12.5): the acknowledgement of §5.7, and the warning names the echo (below). `Firings.fire` with event `DELIVERED` and the mapped event as its value, the actor as the sender said (`{id, login: null, name}`), the delivery id and the depth on the firing. A trigger that is paused (by hand, `ACCOUNT_BROKEN`, `AUTHOR_LEFT`) fires nothing, then or later (package 4, decisions 9 and 10).

**Echoes** (package 4 §5.6 step 3, security review row 10). An agent that writes to the sending software through its tools (a comment on an Azure DevOps work item, a ServiceNow record) makes the sender call the webhook again, and its delivery is not one of Tinfirm's writes by any record the poller could use. So a delivery is an **echo** when its mapped `subject.key` equals the subject key of a conversation of the same trigger (its `place_ref`, or a `system_write` it recorded) that recorded a write within package 4's settle time (`tinfirm.trigger.settle`, `PT90S`) before the delivery was received. An echo's depth is one more than the deepest firing the writing turn took. A depth beyond 3 is `SKIPPED_CHAIN`, as package 4's chain, and a `SYSTEM` message in the writing conversation says that the delivery started nothing. A delivery without a subject key cannot be matched: only `max_firings_per_hour` and the payer's budget bound such a loop. The editor's warning for a webhook trigger says so, and asks for `subject.key` in the mapping.

**The conversation**: place `WEBHOOK` (open point 17), origin `TRIGGER`, `place_ref {systemId, deliveryId, subjectKey}`. With a subject key, one open conversation per trigger and key (`conversation_trigger_webhook_open_uq`): a later delivery about it is an `EVENT` turn, or waits; without one, a conversation per delivery, the trigger's previous idle one ending `SUPERSEDED`, as package 4's schedules do. The prompt's `# Event` (the `trigger` module's `PlacePrompts`) holds, as the sender's data and other people's words: the system, the time, the event, the subject, the actor ("as the sender says, unverified"), the text and the body (cut to 16 KiB, fenced). The actor is never linked to a member and carries no authority (C13, threat model 12.3). **Results stay in Tinfirm**, seen by the trigger's audience, with the optional review; questions and approvals are answered there by the team or the author. **The team and the author write in it**, as in a `SCHEDULE` conversation (package 4, decision 5; open point 9): there is no external thread to keep as the one place, so a message starts a `MESSAGE` turn, or waits. To write elsewhere the agent uses its tools with the table's accounts, under their modes; a callback URL is later (open point 18).

## 10. Open points

Numbered for the owner to decide one by one. Each carries its proposal and how the review of 2026-10-12 left it: *Reviewed: agree* where the reviewer agreed, with the notes the reviewer asked for where it names them; *changed after review* where the proposal itself changed, and how. Point 21 is new.

1. **Slack's transport.** The record does not choose. **Proposal:** both, per system, the admin's choice: the Events API where Tinfirm is reachable from the internet, Socket Mode where it is not (no inbound port; the app-level token's leak is row 20's accepted risk). Socket Mode also avoids a failure of the Events API: Slack disables the event subscriptions of an app whose deliveries keep failing, until someone re-enables them (§5.2), so it suits a deployment whose reachability or database is doubtful. Teams has no such choice and needs a public URL. *Reviewed: agree; the note on disabled subscriptions added, as the reviewer asked.*
2. **Automatic linking on Slack.** The record links automatically "when one identity provider vouches for both"; Slack's `users.info` email is Slack's word, not the company's identity provider's, CONVENTIONS §5 never links by email, and threat model 8.39 says "never by an email or a name". **Proposal:** no automatic link on Slack; the sign-in link once per person, or the member's own Slack sign-in when they connect Slack for `history` (open point 13). The alternative, matching `users.info`'s `profile.email` (scope `users:read.email`) with a member's login email, would need the owner to accept that a Slack workspace admin who changes an email can take over a member's direct messages. *Reviewed: agree.*
3. **Where Teams' claims live.** Row 6 decided `oid` and `tid`; the login keeps only `(issuer, subject)`. **Proposal:** `app_user.oidc_tenant_id` and `oidc_object_id` from each login through an Entra v2.0 issuer whose `tid` matches, read from the ID token itself (§3.8); a provider registered with the v1.0 endpoint links nothing automatically, which the admin guide says; a member who never signed in through Entra links by the sign-in link. *Reviewed: agree; the reviewer's notes on the issuer and the ID token added.*
4. **Threads in direct messages.** **Proposal:** on Slack every top-level direct message starts a conversation answered in its thread (a thread is the conversation, as in channels); Teams' personal chats have no threads, so a member has one open direct-message conversation per Teams system, and "New conversation" or another agent's handle ends it `SUPERSEDED` and starts another. *Reviewed: agree.*
5. **Which agent answers a direct message.** **Proposal:** a handle at the start of the first message, else the member's default (`chat_preference`, set in the app or from the picker), else a picker of the agents they use, holding the message (`AWAITING_AGENT`, an hour) for a press of the same chat user that names it. *Reviewed: agree; the held message's state and the presser's check added, as the reviewer asked.*
6. **The mention grammar.** The record decides one bot per chat system. **Proposal:** the platform's mention of the bot, then an optional text `@handle` (ADR-0034's parser), one default trigger per channel for a bare mention, a thread bound to its agent. One bot per agent is refused: an app and a token per agent per workspace for admins to install and rotate, against the record. *Reviewed: agree.*
7. **Channels of a trigger.** The record is silent on who may attach an agent to a channel. **Proposal:** only channels the bot is in, not shared with another organisation (their replies reach that organisation, threat model 11.5); the author linked and a member of each, so nobody attaches an agent to a private channel they are not in; one trigger per agent per channel and one default. An admin editing on the author's behalf (package 4, decision 7) picks among the author's channels, and every check is the author's. *Reviewed: agree; the admin-on-behalf case added, as the reviewer asked.*
8. **Approval cards in a channel.** **Proposal:** in the thread, with the call in words and a link to the app for its arguments, pressed only by linked members of the team; the review of a result only in the app, so that an unapproved answer is never shown in the channel. *Reviewed: agree.*
9. **Messages from the app into a chat or webhook conversation.** **Proposal:** a `CHAT_THREAD` conversation takes decisions and answers from the app, posted to the thread as "{member} answered in Tinfirm"; plain messages are refused (`PLACE_IS_EXTERNAL`), so the thread stays the one place, as package 4 does for issues. A `WEBHOOK` conversation has no external thread: it takes any message of the trigger's team and author, which starts a `MESSAGE` turn, as package 4's `SCHEDULE` does. *Changed after review: the reviewer agreed for `CHAT_THREAD` and argued against refusing messages on `WEBHOOK`; the `WEBHOOK` half is the reviewer's change.*
10. **Chat content in the app and the audit.** The record says who sees a trigger's conversations, not whether askers do, nor what of the chat is kept. **Proposal:** a channel's conversation is seen in the app by the trigger's audience only, not by the members who asked: the app shows tool results, bindings, runs and transcripts the channel never saw, made with the trigger's accounts. Messages of non-members are shown to that audience with their chat name, since the agent acted on them. The audit keeps ids only; `chat_event` a day, `chat_user` 90 days after last seen; edits and deletions in the chat do not change what a conversation took. *Reviewed: agree.*
11. **Limits** (row 15, threat model Q37). **Proposal:** bodies of 1 MiB, 32 levels and 10 000 values, the operator's, since they protect the server. The rates are company settings that admins change, seeded from the instance's, as package 4's decision 15 does for firings: 20 messages a minute and 200 a day per chat user, 600 events a minute per chat system, webhook deliveries 60 a minute per system by default and 600 at most; a channel or webhook trigger's `max_firings_per_hour` within the company's maximum. A webhook delivery is verified before it counts: only verified deliveries spend the system's rate, and refusals are limited per client address, so a stranger who learns the URL cannot lock the sender out. Every bot's and app's messages are ignored, so two apps cannot loop. *Changed after review: the reviewer argued against instance-only configuration and against counting a webhook's rate before verifying; both are the reviewer's changes.*
12. **Formatting, cards and files.** **Proposal:** Slack's mrkdwn `section` blocks (Slack's newer `markdown` block later, once its limits suit long answers); Teams' Markdown text with Adaptive Cards for cards, their buttons `Action.Submit`, pressed as a message handled like every other event and the card updated through the outbox (§5.8); at most 4 parts, then a file or a link; files in from Slack and out to Slack only. Teams' files are later together: those sent in a personal chat (a pre-authenticated SharePoint `downloadUrl`), uploads (the file consent card) and files in channels (SharePoint through Microsoft Graph); meanwhile the bot says so and links to the conversation in the app. The alternatives: a synchronous `Action.Execute` handler would tell a presser privately at once, at the cost of a second receiving path that calls the platform and decides inside Teams' invoke deadline; downloading a verified personal-chat attachment through a guard exception for `*.sharepoint.com`, for that download only, would serve one of the three Teams file paths with a wildcard for every tenant's SharePoint. *Reviewed: agree; changed after review: Teams buttons are `Action.Submit`, and Teams files in are later too.*
13. **The chat systems' tools** (Part 6: read a channel's history, post). A `LINK` holds no token, and the bot must not act as a tool (C9). **Proposal:** Slack's `history` (`READ`) only, through an `OAUTH` account of a member or a service identity with the Slack app's user scopes `channels:history` and `groups:history`, connected by the usual Connect card; a member's is also their link and replaces a `LINK` only for the same identity (§2.2, §5.4); for `Accounts.bind` a `LINK` is no account to act with. No `post` tool in package 5: a user token's `chat:write` posts as that person, with their name and picture and without "(Tinfirm agent)" (row 16, threat model 11.7), outside the outbox's record, so the bot would read its own posts back as people's words; the write-back already answers in the thread. A later `post` would post through the bot with the label, while the authority check (which channels) uses the conversation's account. Teams' tools later: Microsoft Graph's delegated permissions need the tenant admin's consent. *Changed after review: the reviewer argued against `post` through a user token; it is dropped, `history` kept, and §2.2 now allows the `OAUTH` accounts `history` needs, which it contradicted.* *Amended 2026-10-14:* in a channel trigger's conversation, `history` reads the thread the agent was asked in only (its `place_ref`'s channel and thread), never another thread, the channel's own messages, another channel nor another chat system: anyone in the channel may ask (threat model 11.4), and the account the trigger lends may read far more. Any other read is refused (`TOOL_DENIED`) before Slack is called, audited `tool.denied` at the binding's step; a personal conversation's `history` reads any channel its person may.
14. **The outbox's owner.** Package 4 puts the outbox in the `trigger` module, written with a binding and filled for `TRACKER_ISSUE` only. **Proposal:** `outbox.writer` (`BINDING`, `BOT`) with a nullable `binding_id`; delivery dispatches by kind to writers that modules register (`PlaceWriter`), the `chat` module's for the chat kinds; the `chat` module queues rows for every `CHAT_THREAD` conversation, personal ones included. *Reviewed: agree.*
15. **The webhook's signature** (row 14, threat model Q38). **Proposal:** the Standard Webhooks scheme (`webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature`), the HMAC keyed with the decoded secret (the bytes after `whsec_`), a window of 300 seconds either way, ids remembered 7 days, the previous secret accepted 24 hours after a rotation; `SECRET_HEADER` for senders that cannot sign, marked weaker, with what it loses on the system's page. Body-only HMACs (GitHub's `X-Hub-Signature-256`, Linear's) arrive with package 4's poll hints. *Reviewed: agree; changed after review: the decoded key, and 300 seconds ahead instead of 30, the record's five minutes and the libraries' tolerance.*
16. **An admin removing a member's chat account.** Package 1's I4 lets only the member manage their accounts. **Proposal:** admins may remove a member's account on a chat system, a `LINK` or the `OAUTH` account that serves as the link (open point 13), as a kill switch for a compromised chat account (R45), audited; the member may connect again. *Reviewed: agree; changed after review: the first draft kept a member's `OAUTH` chat account out of reach, which would have left a compromised chat account linked through it.*
17. **The webhook's place.** **Proposal:** a place value `WEBHOOK`, with one open conversation per trigger and mapped subject key, else one per delivery; results in Tinfirm as for `SCHEDULE`. Reusing `SCHEDULE` would fit the results but not its `place_ref` or its one-conversation-at-rest rule. *Reviewed: agree.*
18. **A callback URL for a webhook's results.** **Proposal:** later: it is a write to a URL a sender chose, with the trigger's results, and needs its own signing and the outbound guard; meanwhile the agent writes back through tools of the table's systems. *Reviewed: agree.*
19. **Ending a chat conversation.** The record names no end. **Proposal:** no idle end; it ends by its person (an "End" button on each answer, or the app), its team, author or an admin, its trigger deleted, its person leaving, its agent deleted, or its system removed (`SYSTEM_REMOVED`); a channel's conversation whose author leaves waits for a new author instead (package 4, decision 6); a mention in an ended thread starts nothing and says so. *Reviewed: agree; changed after review: a channel's or webhook's conversation no longer ends when its author leaves, per package 4's decision 6.*
20. **The agent's picture in a chat.** Row 16 says a post's avatar is the agent's, but agents have no avatar (package 3), and Slack's `icon_url` must be a public URL Slack fetches, which a Socket Mode deployment may not have. **Proposal:** the bot's own picture with the agent's name and "(Tinfirm agent)" in package 5, which is never a person's; an avatar per agent, chosen by its creator and served publicly, later. *Reviewed: agree.*
21. **The bot's failure, and package 4's decision 9.** Package 4 decided that an account that cannot be used pauses the whole trigger (`ACCOUNT_BROKEN`), resumed only by hand, and that nothing of the meantime fires. The bot is in no trigger's table, yet every channel trigger of its system stops when it is removed, refused or its service identity suspended. Two options:
    - **(a) Pause.** The system's channel triggers pause `BOT_BROKEN`; once `PUT …/bot` passes, or the service identity is resumed, each is resumed by hand, by its author or an admin.
    - **(b) A deliberate exception: wait.** The triggers wait (`BOT_MISSING`, `BOT_RECONNECT`, `BOT_SUSPENDED`) and go on by themselves once the bot is usable; the mentions of the gap are dropped unanswered and never answered later, so nothing bursts (decision 10).

    **Proposal:** (b), which the body follows (§3.10, §5.14). Decision 9 guards a trigger's authority: an account of its table that broke may have been revoked on purpose, so a person confirms before the trigger acts with it again. The bot carries no authority (C9: never a binding, a tool's credential or a payer); it is shared infrastructure outside every table, and every way back is already an admin's deliberate act on it (`PUT …/bot` with a token that passes, reinstalling the app, resuming its service identity). Under (a) that one act would have to be followed by a resume of every channel trigger of the workspace, by their many authors, who can neither fix the bot nor change anything by resuming. Under both, nothing said during the gap is answered later. *New after review.*
