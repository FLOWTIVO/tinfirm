# ADR-0083: A Gmail add-on whose HTTP endpoints Tinfirm serves, acting as the person who linked their Google account

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-20
- Decided by: the owner, 2026-10-20
- Amended by: [ADR-0084](0084-email-attachments-and-gmail-drafts.md), under which the add-on sends the email's attachments as files of the conversation (decision 5), saves an answer as a draft reply in the thread with **Use as reply**, and its deployment asks for `gmail.addons.current.action.compose` and may open Gmail's links (decision 8)
- Sources: [PLATFORM.md](../PLATFORM.md) §1, §8.1; [CONVENTIONS.md](../CONVENTIONS.md) §4 (paths, configuration), §5 (people are identified by issuer and subject, never by email), §14.4 (the outbound client), §14.11, §15.16 (linking); [ADR-0045](0045-mcp-server-for-peoples-clients.md) (people's AI clients); [ADR-0074](0074-outlook-add-in.md) and [ADR-0076](0076-outlook-pane-thread-and-roaming-links.md) (the Outlook add-in); [ADR-0075](0075-companies-and-sign-in-from-the-deployment.md) (configuration is the deployment's); the package 5 contract, §5.4 (the chat sign-in link); Google's Workspace add-ons with HTTP endpoints (alternate runtimes)
- Changes: the `mcp` module, which serves the add-on's endpoints and owns its tables (`V056__gmail_add_on.sql`); the `system` module (the port `GmailCalls`: Google's signing keys and the Gmail API); the shared `BearerGrant` (the kind `GMAIL_LINK`); the API (tag `Gmail`); the web app (the route `/gmail/link/{companyId}`, the add-on in Settings → General and the person's links on their profile); the settings `TINFIRM_GMAIL_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` and `TINFIRM_GMAIL_CLIENT_ID`

## Context

- People who read their email in Gmail want what the Outlook add-in gives (ADR-0074): ask one of the company's agents to summarise, triage, investigate or draft a reply to the email they have open.
- Gmail extends its sidebar with **Google Workspace add-ons**. Such an add-on is either an Apps Script project or a set of **HTTP endpoints**: Google POSTs an event to the endpoint (the open message's id, a per-message token, the person's OAuth token and ID token, the card's form inputs) and shows the card the endpoint answers. The add-on draws no page of its own: its UI is Google's cards (text, buttons, a dropdown, text inputs, links that open a window), which do not refresh themselves.
- Google signs an ID token for each request: in the `Authorization` header, an ID token of the deployment's service account (`service-{project number}@gcp-sa-gsuiteaddons.iam.gserviceaccount.com`) whose audience is the endpoint's URL; in the event, the person's ID token, whose audience is the deployment's OAuth client. `gcloud workspace-add-ons get-authorization` names both for a Google Cloud project. The add-on reads the open message with the Gmail API, the person's OAuth token and the event's per-message token (`X-Goog-Gmail-Access-Token`), under the scope `gmail.addons.current.message.readonly`, which reaches that message only.
- A deployment is a JSON file that a Google Workspace administrator creates in a Google Cloud project (`gcloud workspace-add-ons deployments create`), installs for themselves to try, and publishes to their domain through the Google Workspace Marketplace SDK.
- Tinfirm links an outside identity to a person only through a sign-in that names it (the chat sign-in link, package 5 §5.4), never by email address.

## Decision

1. **A Workspace add-on with HTTP endpoints that Tinfirm serves.** Not an Apps Script project and not a browser extension. Google calls `POST /api/gmail/{companyId}/home` (Gmail with no email open), `/open` (an email opened) and `/act` (a button of a card), outside `/api/companies` and the session chain, without cookies or CSRF, as the chat platforms' endpoints are. The add-on is part of every installation at its `TINFIRM_PUBLIC_URL`, which Google must reach from the internet.
2. **Every request is Google's, for this deployment, for a Google account.** The endpoint verifies, before it reads anything else, Google's two RS256 ID tokens against Google's signing keys (`https://www.googleapis.com/oauth2/v3/certs`, through the outbound client, kept a day and read again for an unknown key at most once a minute): issuer `https://accounts.google.com`, not expired (five minutes' skew); the service account's token with the audience the endpoint's URL under `TINFIRM_PUBLIC_URL` and the email `TINFIRM_GMAIL_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`, verified; the person's token with the audience `TINFIRM_GMAIL_CLIENT_ID`. The person's token gives the Google account: its subject, and its email for the person to recognise. A request that fails is answered 401 and logged with why, never with a token. Without the two settings, the endpoints answer 404.
3. **A Google account acts as the person who linked it.** An unlinked account gets a card with **Sign in to Tinfirm**, which opens `/gmail/link/{companyId}#{token}`: a sign-in link of 10 minutes and one use (32 random bytes, kept as their SHA-256), as the chat's. After the Tinfirm sign-in, the page names the Google account (its email, as Google asserted it) and when the link was asked for, warns to confirm only a link the person just asked for in Gmail, says who reads these conversations, and links on the person's confirmation only. A Google account is linked to one person per company at most; a person may link several. A new link replaces the account's earlier, unused one. The link ends with **Sign out** in the add-on, **Disconnect** on the person's profile, after 30 days without use, or when the person is no longer an active member, which every request checks. Linking and unlinking are audited.
4. **It acts as an AI client does.** A request of a linked account runs as its person, through the same services as the MCP endpoint's tools (ADR-0045): the person's own conversations only (the `CLIENT` view), a start with the origin `CLIENT`, the person's accounts and budget, the AI clients' hourly limit of starts (`TINFIRM_MCP_STARTS_PER_HOUR`), the Owner's notice on the start card, and no decision. Its credential is a `BearerGrant` of the kind `GMAIL_LINK` with the scope `tinfirm:write`, so the audit of its writes carries `gmailLinkId`. It adds no tool to the endpoint.
5. **It reads the open email only when the person asks.** Opening an email reads nothing from Gmail. A start reads that one message with the Gmail API (`format=full`), then forgets the tokens; they are never stored or logged. The request is built as the Outlook pane builds it: the instruction, then the subject, sender, recipients, date and plain-text body (the text of the HTML part when there is no plain one), fenced as other people's words with a random tag and cut to the 100,000 characters of a message. Attachments are not sent.
6. **Cards.** The start card shows the email's subject, the agents the person may use (the last one chosen first), **Summarise**, **Triage**, **Investigate**, **Draft a reply**, and a question in the person's own words. The conversation card shows the conversation oldest first (the request as its action, never the email; the agent's answers and questions; the person's follow-ups; Tinfirm's notes), the newest ten messages at most, each cut at 2,000 characters, where it stands, **Refresh**, a reply (which answers the open questions while one is open), **Open in Tinfirm** and **Ask something else**. Cards do not refresh themselves: **Refresh**, and opening the email again, show the conversation as it stands. The agent's Markdown is shown as text, with bold and line breaks kept.
7. **Tinfirm remembers each email's conversation, per person.** Under a hash of Gmail's message id, and of its thread id (the first 32 hex digits of SHA-256), the conversation, the action and when it started, for the person's last 100 emails, deleted with the conversation. Opening the email again, or another message of its thread, shows that conversation. This is the reference ADR-0076 left for a client other than Outlook, kept by the add-on and not by the conversation: `start_conversation` and the REST API are unchanged.
8. **A deployment file per company.** `GET /api/companies/{companyId}/gmail/deployment` (any member; nothing in it is secret) answers the deployment's JSON: the scopes `gmail.addons.execute`, `gmail.addons.current.message.readonly` and `userinfo.email`, the homepage and the contextual trigger at the company's endpoints, the public URL as the one prefix links may open, and the logo. Settings → General offers it with the steps. `TINFIRM_GMAIL_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` and `TINFIRM_GMAIL_CLIENT_ID` are the deployment's configuration (ADR-0075), one Google Cloud project for the instance: each company's deployment lives in it.

## Consequences

- Gmail's sidebar reaches the company's agents with one deployment, without a service of its own, with the rules of the AI clients: the person's role, accounts, budget and limits, and the Owner's notice.
- Tinfirm must be reachable from Google at its public URL, and must reach `www.googleapis.com` and `gmail.googleapis.com`. An installation on a private network cannot serve it.
- A person sees a turn's progress by choosing **Refresh**: a card cannot poll as the Outlook pane does.
- Tinfirm keeps per person and email two hashes, a conversation's id, an action and a time, and per link the Google account's subject and email. The email itself is in the conversation's first message, as from Outlook.
- A link sent by someone else, and confirmed by a person who did not read the page, would let that someone's Google account act as the person. The page names the Google account, warns, and the link lasts 10 minutes; the threat model records it.
- Not in this step: **Use as reply** (a draft in the thread, which needs the compose scope), attachments, compose-time actions, and email as an event source of triggers.

## Alternatives considered

- **An Apps Script add-on.** A second code base in another language, outside Tinfirm's tests, created by hand in each company's Apps Script project, signing in to Tinfirm's authorization server with a redirect URI per script. Rejected.
- **A browser extension that draws into Gmail's page.** One browser at a time, a store's review, and it breaks when Gmail's page changes. Rejected.
- **Tinfirm's OAuth grant as the link.** The add-on's backend is Tinfirm itself, which would hold tokens to itself, and the consent page names the client, not the Google account, which is what tells a person that a link someone sent them is not theirs. Rejected.
- **Linking by the Google account's email.** Tinfirm never links people by email address (CONVENTIONS §5). Rejected.
- **The Google Cloud project's names as company settings in the app.** Configuration belongs to the deployment (ADR-0075). Rejected.
