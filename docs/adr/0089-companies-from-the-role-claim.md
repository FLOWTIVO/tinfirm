# ADR-0089: One identity provider fills several companies from its role claim, and membership follows the claim at every sign-in

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-24
- Decided by: the owner, 2026-10-24
- Sources: [CONVENTIONS.md](../CONVENTIONS.md) §4, §5 (a provider's company, a provider's role), §6, §8; [ADR-0075](0075-companies-and-sign-in-from-the-deployment.md) decisions 3 and 4; [ADR-0077](0077-licence-editions-and-seats.md) decisions 4 and 5; [ADR-0086](0086-saml-sign-in.md) decision 11; [ADR-0087](0087-scim-provisioning.md) decisions 6 and 7; [ADR-0088](0088-roles-from-the-identity-provider.md); the [threat model](../threat-model.md) 3.16
- Amends: [ADR-0075](0075-companies-and-sign-in-from-the-deployment.md) decision 4 (a registration fills one company; a known identity is never granted a membership again) and [ADR-0088](0088-roles-from-the-identity-provider.md) decision 2 (the role applies to the membership of the provider's one company; a sign-in never grants a membership) for providers that decide
- Changes: the identity module's provider settings (`AuthProperties.OidcProvider.companies`, `SamlProperties.Provider.companies`, `ProviderRoles.RoleMap`), the resolution of a provider's companies (`ProviderCompanies`), the provisioning (`ExternalProvisioning.applyClaim`), the port `ProvisionedMemberships` (`leave`, `releaseProviderRoles` with a set of keys per provider) and its implementation in the company module, which enters each company's tenant context; the sign-in event (`UserEvents.UserSignedIn.providerCompanyIds`); the Helm chart (`auth.oidc.providers[].companies`, `auth.saml.providers[].companies`); no migration

## Context

- A registration of the instance fills one declared company (ADR-0075 decision 4): its `_COMPANY`, or the only declared company. A company with people from two identity providers needs two registrations, and a provider that should fill two companies needs two registrations too, each with its own client at the IdP and its own redirect URI. A person who works for two companies gets an account from one provider and is added to the other company by hand.
- ADR-0088 made the provider decide a member's *role* at every sign-in, from a mapped claim, so that a change at the IdP reaches Tinfirm. The provider could not decide the *membership*: a sign-in never granted one again, a person removed in the app stayed removed, and a person the IdP moved out of every mapped group could not sign in but kept their membership. ADR-0088 rejected removal on an unmapped claim because a misconfigured group would start departures for a whole department.
- The companies could be defined by the roles, so that the IdP's groups say which companies a person is in, with which role. An instance with several companies is one operator's, usually with one identity provider for all of them.
- A membership already carries its own role and role source per company (`company_membership`), so one person can hold different roles in different companies, and a provider's role source names its registration (`IDP:<id>`). Nothing in the data model binds a registration to one company; only the provisioning policy does.

## Decision

**A provider's role claim may fill several declared companies. Beside its own company and its `_ROLE_MAP_*`, a provider lists, per further company, the claim's values that give each role there. When the provider decides (`_ROLE_SOURCE=IDP`), a person is a member of each of the provider's companies the claim names at every sign-in, with the role mapped there: named in a company they are no member of, they join it; named in none of the provider's companies any more where they are a member, they leave it, as a departure. A claim that names no company at all refuses the sign-in and changes nothing. OWNER and SCIM-managed memberships stay out of the provider's reach, as before. The same for OIDC and SAML.**

1. **The settings**, per provider, beside `_COMPANY`, `_ROLE`, `_ROLE_CLAIM`, `_ROLE_MAP_*` and `_ROLE_SOURCE` (`TINFIRM_AUTH_OIDC_PROVIDERS_<ID>_*` and `TINFIRM_AUTH_SAML_PROVIDERS_<ID>_*`; Helm `companies` on each provider, a list of `key` and `roleMap`):

   | Setting | Meaning | Default |
   |---|---|---|
   | `_COMPANIES_<KEY>_ROLE_MAP_ADMIN`, `_COMPANIES_<KEY>_ROLE_MAP_MEMBER`, `_COMPANIES_<KEY>_ROLE_MAP_VIEWER` | The claim's values, comma-separated and matched exactly, that give each role in the declared company `<KEY>`; the highest wins there, as in `_ROLE_MAP_*` | none |

   `<KEY>` is a declared company's key (ADR-0075 decision 1), so that it survives an environment variable's name. The provider's own company (`_COMPANY`, or the only declared company) keeps `_ROLE_MAP_*`; the same key under `_COMPANIES_*` stops the start. One value may give a role in several companies. The server does not start on `_COMPANIES_*` without `_ROLE_CLAIM`, an undeclared key, a key that is not a key, a company without any map, an empty value, or a value under two roles of one company; nor on a deciding provider whose own company has no `_ROLE_MAP_*` while `_COMPANIES_*` is set, since it would make nobody a member of its own company. Under `APP` the own company's new members take `_ROLE` when the claim maps none there, as before, so no map is needed there.

2. **The provider's companies.** A provider fills its own company and each company of `_COMPANIES_*`. The server resolves them from the deployment's settings before the sign-in, never from the request (`ProviderCompanies`); a declared company that does not exist yet, because it waits for a licence (ADR-0077 decision 4), is left out until it does. The sign-in runs as `SYSTEM` in the tenant context of the provider's own company, else the first of its companies, and the company module enters each company's context before it writes that company's membership (`TenantScope.enterCompany`, as the declared companies' start does), so row-level security admits each write for its company only, and each audit event lands in its company's chain.

3. **The provider decides (`IDP`): membership follows the claim.** At every sign-in through the provider, of a new user or a known identity, the server maps the claim's values in each of the provider's companies and, in the sign-in's transaction, as actor `SYSTEM`:
   - **A role mapped where the person is a member**: ADR-0088 decision 2 unchanged, `member.role.changed` when it changes, with the seat fallback.
   - **A role mapped where the person is no member**: the membership is granted, `member.added` with `userId`, `role`, `provider` and `roleSource: IDP`, with the seat fallback and a principal of their own (ADR-0075 decision 4 and ADR-0088 decision 2 amended: a sign-in through a deciding provider grants a membership the claim names). A member the company removed in the app comes back at their next sign-in while the IdP still names them: under `IDP` the IdP is where people are removed, or the provider is set to `APP`.
   - **No role mapped where the person is a member**: a departure. The person leaves the company as a member an admin removes does (the package 6 contract, §5.5; ADR-0087 decision 7): their principal becomes `LEFT`, their content stays, the departure's checklist goes to the Owners and Admins, and `member.removed` carries `provider` and `roleSource: IDP`. The departure runs under the company's lock, as the member operations of the app and SCIM do, and reads the membership after it, so a promotion to OWNER that commits meanwhile is seen and kept; a sign-in takes its companies' locks in one order on every replica.
   - **No role mapped in any of the provider's companies**: the sign-in is refused, `ROLE_NOT_MAPPED`, as ADR-0088 decision 2 says, and nothing changes: no departure, no user created. A departure needs the claim to still name the person somewhere, so a group misconfigured at the IdP, or a claim that goes missing, refuses sign-ins instead of emptying companies, and everything stays recoverable. An OWNER's membership, or one SCIM manages, keeps them in: they sign in as they are.
   - **OWNER is never the provider's** (ADR-0088 decision 4): never made, changed, refused or removed. A membership whose role source is `SCIM` is never changed or ended by a sign-in (ADR-0088 decision 8).
   - **One deciding provider per company** (ADR-0088 decision 2) now counts every company a provider fills: the server does not start when two deciding providers, of either kind, share a company, whether as their own or through the claim.
   - **The declared owner** (ADR-0075 decision 3): a sign-in through the provider that asserts the person's email may make them the declared OWNER of any company the provider fills.
4. **The app decides (`APP`).** A new user joins the provider's own company with the mapped role, else `_ROLE`, and each further company whose map names them, with the role mapped there, once, with the seat fallback, role source `APP`. A known identity only signs in; no membership is granted again or ended. ADR-0075 decision 4 stands for `APP`.
5. **Switching, releasing.** ADR-0088 decision 7 holds per company: at the start, a membership whose role source is a provider that no longer decides *that* company's roles (the provider switched to `APP`, was removed, or dropped the company from `_COMPANY` or `_COMPANIES_*`) becomes `APP`, keeping its role, audited `member.role_source.changed` with `count`. A company added to a provider's `_COMPANIES_*` takes its members at their next sign-ins.
6. **What a company sees.** Settings → Sign-in of a company lists every provider that fills it, as its own company or through the claim (`GET /api/companies/{companyId}/sign-in`), with the same fields as before. Nothing changes in the web app.
7. **Edition.** Several declared companies need Enterprise (ADR-0077 decision 4), so `_COMPANIES_*` has an effect under Enterprise only; its settings are read and checked in every edition, in the core, as ADR-0088's are.
8. **Testing.** `ProviderRolesTest`: a further company's map, its problems. `AuthPropertiesTest`: the settings from environment variables, an undeclared key, the provider's own key under `_COMPANIES_*`, a deciding provider without a map for its own company, a provider deciding each company its claim fills and two deciding providers sharing one. `ExternalProvisioningTest` and `SamlProvisioningTest`: a new user joining each named company, a known one joining and leaving, a claim that names none refusing and ending nothing, an OWNER kept, `APP` once, a provider without an own company. `OidcLoginIntegrationTest.theProviderFillsEachCompanyItsClaimNames`: against the database and row-level security, a person in two companies with two roles, the departure audited with the provider, the return, the refusal keeping both memberships, both companies' sign-in pages.

## Consequences

- An instance with several companies and one identity provider needs one registration: the IdP's groups say who is in which company, with which role. A person in two companies is in both from their first sign-in, with one account.
- Under `IDP`, the IdP now decides membership too. Removing a member on **Members** is undone at their next sign-in while the IdP still names them; the guides say to remove people at the IdP, or to set the provider to `APP`. That also holds for a provider of one company that decides roles, which is the common setup: a person the company removed in the app comes back while the IdP maps them.
- A person moved out of one company's groups at the IdP leaves that company at their next sign-in, with the departure's checklist for its Owners and Admins; moved out of every mapped group, they cannot sign in and keep everything, as ADR-0088 decided.
- The IdP's group administration decides membership and departures as well as roles (threat model 3.16).
- CONVENTIONS changes: §4 (the setting, OIDC and SAML), §5 (a provider's companies, membership following the claim, the departure), §8 (`member.removed` with `provider` and `roleSource`, `member.added` for a known identity).

## Alternatives considered

- **The company in each map value** (`_ROLE_MAP_ADMIN=acme:acme-admins,globex:globex-admins`). One variable, but a separator inside values that are group names or ids, and no place for a company without an admin group. The key in the variable's name follows `TINFIRM_COMPANIES_<KEY>_*`.
- **Keep a membership the claim names no more.** The IdP could add people but never remove them; the person would keep reading the company's conversations. The owner chose the departure.
- **Demote to VIEWER instead of a departure.** A Viewer still reads what is shared with their groups, and holds a seat's worth of access to the company's results. Not taken.
- **End every membership when the claim names no company.** Rejected as ADR-0088 rejected it: a misconfigured group or a missing claim would empty companies at their people's next sign-ins. Refusing keeps everything recoverable.
- **Keep "a sign-in never grants a membership again" under `IDP`.** The IdP could then remove a person from a company but never add one who already has an account, which is half of defining the companies by the roles. Under `APP` the rule stands.
- **A registration per company, as before.** It works, at the cost of a client and a redirect URI per company at the IdP, and of a person with two accounts' worth of buttons on the login page.
