# ADR-0088: The identity provider decides a member's role by default, at every sign-in, for OIDC and SAML alike; the app decides when the provider is set so

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-22
- Decided by: the owner, 2026-10-22
- Sources: [CONVENTIONS.md](../CONVENTIONS.md) §4, §5 (a provider's company, the declared owner), §6 (authorization matrix, seats, `LAST_OWNER`), §8, §15.24 (the seat check, `SEAT_FALLBACK`); [ADR-0058](0058-authority-from-the-audience.md) (the roles); [ADR-0075](0075-companies-and-sign-in-from-the-deployment.md) decision 4; [ADR-0077](0077-licence-editions-and-seats.md) decision 5; [ADR-0086](0086-saml-sign-in.md); [ADR-0087](0087-scim-provisioning.md); the [threat model](../threat-model.md), 3.10
- Amended by: [ADR-0089](0089-companies-from-the-role-claim.md): a provider fills several companies from its claim; under `IDP` membership follows the claim at every sign-in (a membership the claim names is granted, one it names no more is a departure), so decision 2's "a sign-in never grants a membership again" no longer holds for a deciding provider; "one provider decides per company" counts every company a provider fills
- Amends: [ADR-0075](0075-companies-and-sign-in-from-the-deployment.md) decision 4 (a provider's `_ROLE`, given once to a new user); [ADR-0086](0086-saml-sign-in.md) decision 6 and [ADR-0087](0087-scim-provisioning.md) decisions 4 and 7, which follow it

## Context

- Under ADR-0075 decision 4 a provider gives a new user one fixed role, `_ROLE`, and never again: "a known identity only signs in, and no membership is granted again". A person whom the identity provider moves into the admins' group, or out of the company's users, keeps the role Tinfirm gave them at their first sign-in until an Owner or Admin changes it by hand.
- The company's identity provider is where its IT already decides who does what. A company expects a change there to reach every application, as Entra ID's app roles and Okta's groups do elsewhere.
- Roles are per company, and a seat follows the role (ADR-0077 decision 5). A provider is bound to one declared company (ADR-0075), so a provider can only ever speak for that company's membership.
- OWNER is special: it comes from the deployment's declared owner or from the app, it governs retention, erasure and the other Owners, and the last active OWNER cannot be removed (`LAST_OWNER`).
- SCIM (ADR-0087) already pushes a role attribute; SAML (ADR-0086) proposed a role attribute for a new user only and left the rest open.

## Decision

**Every provider, OIDC and SAML, has a role source. By default the provider decides: the role comes from a mapped claim or attribute, at every sign-in, and the app cannot change it. Set to the app, the provider gives a role once, as ADR-0075 does today. A claim that maps to no role refuses the sign-in. OWNER is never the provider's. SCIM is the same rule without waiting for a sign-in.**

1. **The settings**, per provider, beside `_COMPANY`, `_ROLE` and `_EMAIL_DOMAINS` (`TINFIRM_AUTH_OIDC_PROVIDERS_<ID>_*` and `TINFIRM_AUTH_SAML_PROVIDERS_<ID>_*`; Helm `roleSource`, `roleClaim`, `roleMap` on each provider):

   | Setting | Meaning | Default |
   |---|---|---|
   | `_ROLE_SOURCE` | `IDP`: the provider decides the role at every sign-in. `APP`: the provider gives a new user a role once, and the app decides after | `IDP` (but see decision 6 for a provider without `_ROLE_CLAIM`) |
   | `_ROLE_CLAIM` | The OIDC claim (from the ID token and the userinfo response, as the email claims are read) or the SAML attribute that carries the role's values, a string or an array: for example `groups`, `roles`, or `http://schemas.microsoft.com/ws/2008/06/identity/claims/role` | none |
   | `_ROLE_MAP_ADMIN`, `_ROLE_MAP_MEMBER`, `_ROLE_MAP_VIEWER` | The claim's values, comma-separated and matched exactly, that give each role: for example Entra ID's app role values `Tinfirm.Admin`, `Tinfirm.Member`, `Tinfirm.Viewer`, or Okta's group names | none |
   | `_ROLE` | Under `APP`, a new user's role when the claim maps none or no claim is set; under `IDP`, see decision 6 | `MEMBER` |

   There is no value for OWNER, and a claim's value that names OWNER is never read as a role. When the claim holds values of several roles, the highest wins: ADMIN, then MEMBER, then VIEWER. The server does not start on a `_ROLE_MAP_*` without a `_ROLE_CLAIM`, an empty value, a value listed under two roles, or `_ROLE_SOURCE` other than `IDP` or `APP`. ADR-0086's `_ROLE_ATTRIBUTE` and `_ROLE_VALUES_*` become these settings, the same for OIDC and SAML.

2. **The provider decides (`IDP`).** At **every** sign-in through the provider, of a new user or a known identity, the server reads the role from the claim and applies it to the user's membership of the provider's company, in the sign-in's transaction:
   - **The role changes** when it differs: `member.role.changed` by `SYSTEM`, with `provider`, `roleSource: IDP`, the role before and after. A promotion and a demotion apply alike.
   - **The seat rule stays** (ADR-0077 decision 5): a role that holds a seat, for a person without one while none is free, gives VIEWER, with `seatFallback: true` and the role asked, and the Owners and Admins get the `SEAT_FALLBACK` item (one per person, while it lasts); each later sign-in asks again, so the role is given once a seat is free. Signing in never fails for want of a seat.
   - **A claim that maps to no role refuses the sign-in**: no value of the claim is listed in `_ROLE_MAP_*`, or the claim is missing. `/login?error=ROLE_NOT_MAPPED`, audited `auth.login.failed` with reason `role_not_mapped` and the provider. A new user is not created. A known user's membership, role and content stay as they are; they cannot sign in through that provider until the identity provider maps them again, and their sessions end at the idle timeout or the absolute lifetime, as for any user removed at the IdP (threat model 3.6). Removing the person from Tinfirm stays the admins' act, or SCIM's (ADR-0087).
   - **The membership is the provider's**: it records its role source, the provider's registration id (`company_member.role_source`). **One provider decides per company:** the server does not start when two providers of one company are `IDP` (a second registration of the same issuer included), since the same identity could sign in through either and each map would undo the other's role. A sign-in through an `APP` provider never changes a role, and neither does one through the provider of another company. A sign-in never grants a membership again (ADR-0075 decision 4 stands): a member the company removed still signs in to the instance, but is no member of that company, whose paths answer them `404`.
3. **The app cannot change a provider's role.** While a membership's role source is a provider, changing its role in the app (`PATCH /members/{userId}`, the instance admin's break-glass included) is `409 ROLE_MANAGED` with `details.provider`. The members page shows the role as "managed by <provider's name>", with no role picker. Everything else stays the app's: adding the user to another company, suspending, removing (a departure), erasing, groups and shares. **Making a managed member OWNER** in the app is allowed, as OWNER is never the provider's (decision 4): the membership's role source becomes `APP`, and stays so while they are OWNER.
4. **OWNER is never the provider's**, whatever the mode. A provider never makes an OWNER and never demotes, refuses or removes one: at an OWNER's sign-in the role claim is not applied and a claim that maps to no role does not refuse them, so that a group misconfigured at the IdP cannot lock a company out of its owners. The declared owner (ADR-0075 decision 3) still becomes OWNER as before. An OWNER demoted in the app to another role becomes the provider's again at their next sign-in under `IDP`.
5. **The app decides (`APP`).** ADR-0075 decision 4, unchanged: a new user gets the role the claim maps (`_ROLE_CLAIM` and `_ROLE_MAP_*`, when set) or else `_ROLE`, with the seat fallback, once; a known identity only signs in; the app changes the role after. The membership's role source is `APP`.
6. **Providers configured before this decision.** A provider with `_ROLE` and no `_ROLE_CLAIM` has nothing to decide with. Decided by the owner, 2026-10-23: **such a provider runs as `APP`**, whatever its default, and the start logs once that its roles stay the app's until `_ROLE_CLAIM` is set; an explicit `_ROLE_SOURCE=IDP` without `_ROLE_CLAIM` stops the start, with a message that names the setting. The default `IDP` applies to every provider that names a claim. Upgrading changes nothing, and the Helm chart and the guides set a claim and its map for every new provider, so a new install is provider-decides.
7. **Switching a provider's mode** is a change of the deployment and a restart. To `IDP`: each membership takes the provider's role at the person's next sign-in (an Admin made in the app is demoted then if the IdP says Member; the guides say so). To `APP`: at the start, every membership whose role source is that provider becomes `APP`, keeping its role, audited once per company (`member.role_source.changed`, a count). The same holds for SCIM (decision 8): when a company's `_SCIM_ROLE_SOURCE` becomes `APP`, or its SCIM is no longer allowed, every membership whose role source is `scim` becomes `APP` at the start, keeping its role, and sign-in claims apply to it again as its provider's mode says. **When the licence stops serving SCIM** (ADR-0087 decision 10), which happens without a restart, the same release runs at once, on the licence's change (`LicenceChanged`, after the hourly pass or a key applied), so that the company's admins can change those roles while SCIM is stopped; when a key serves SCIM again, SCIM takes each role back at its next write that carries one.
8. **SCIM follows the same rule** (ADR-0087), with the company's own setting `TINFIRM_COMPANIES_<KEY>_SCIM_ROLE_SOURCE`, `IDP` by default:
   - Under `IDP`, SCIM owns the role of the memberships it manages: the role attribute (`urn:ietf:params:scim:schemas:extension:tinfirm:2.0:User:role`) applies whenever a request carries it, with the seat fallback, at once and without a sign-in; the membership's role source is `scim`, and the app's lock of decision 3 applies ("managed by SCIM"). A request without the attribute leaves the role as it is, so that Okta's `PUT` of a whole user without a role mapping never resets it. A new member whose request carries none takes `_SCIM_ROLE`.
   - Under `APP`, SCIM gives the role once, at creation; later role attributes are ignored and answered as stored.
   - **SCIM wins over sign-in.** A membership whose role source is `scim` is not changed by a sign-in's claim, and a claim that maps to no role does not refuse its sign-in: SCIM already removes access at once by deactivation, and two writers of one role would undo each other.
   - OWNER is SCIM's as decision 4 says: never made, never demoted (ADR-0087 decision 7).
9. **Where the code lives.** The core, in every edition: the role source and its lock are OIDC's too, which is Community's. `ExternalProvisioning` (ADR-0086 decision 11) reads the role for both kinds; SAML's caller stays under `ee/`, SCIM's under `ee/`.
10. **Testing.** `OidcLoginIntegrationTest` and the SAML tests of ADR-0086 gain: a promotion and a demotion at a later sign-in; the seat fallback at a sign-in, then the role given at the next one once a seat is free; `ROLE_NOT_MAPPED` for a new user and a known one, the membership kept; an OWNER neither demoted nor refused; `409 ROLE_MANAGED` in the app, an OWNER made in the app; a provider without `_ROLE_CLAIM` running as `APP`, an explicit `IDP` without one refusing the start; a switch to `APP` releasing the memberships. `ScimIntegrationTest` gains SCIM's role under `IDP` and `APP` and SCIM winning over a sign-in. Keycloak in CI (ADR-0086 decision 16) maps a group to each role, for both OIDC and SAML.

## Consequences

- A change at the identity provider reaches Tinfirm: at the person's next sign-in for OIDC and SAML, at once with SCIM.
- Under `IDP` the company's Owners and Admins no longer choose its members' roles in the app; they ask the IdP's admin. An Owner who wants to keep choosing sets the provider to `APP`.
- A person moved out of every mapped group cannot sign in, but keeps their membership and their data until someone removes them; existing sessions run out at their limits. A company that needs removal at once uses SCIM.
- Whoever administers the IdP's groups now grants ADMIN in Tinfirm at every sign-in, not only at the first: the IdP's group administration becomes part of Tinfirm's trust boundary (threat model 3.16).
- Under `IDP`, a promotion an Admin made in the app before the switch is undone at the person's next sign-in when the IdP says otherwise.
- OWNER stays the app's and the deployment's; no IdP configuration can take a company's ownership.
- CONVENTIONS changes when built: §4 (the four settings, OIDC and SAML, and `TINFIRM_COMPANIES_<KEY>_SCIM_ROLE_SOURCE`), §5 (a provider's role, the refusal `ROLE_NOT_MAPPED`, an OWNER's sign-in), §6 (`409 ROLE_MANAGED`, the members page's rule for a managed role), §8 (`member.role.changed` with `roleSource`, `member.role_source.changed`).

## Alternatives considered

- **The app decides by default.** Upgrades would need nothing, and Admins keep their picker; but a change at the IdP would still not reach Tinfirm unless each operator opts in. The owner chose the provider.
- **A claim that maps to no role gives VIEWER** instead of refusing. It keeps the person reading the company's conversations after the IdP took them out of every group. The owner chose to refuse.
- **A claim that maps to no role removes or suspends the member.** A misconfigured group would start departures for a whole department at their next sign-ins. Refusing keeps everything recoverable.
- **The provider may also grant OWNER.** An IdP group would then control retention, erasure and the other Owners, and a group misconfiguration could leave a company without one. Not taken.
- **The role at sign-in only for SAML, as ADR-0086 first proposed.** Two rules for two kinds of provider; the owner's concern was OIDC's.

## The owner's decision on the open point

**Providers configured before this decision** (decision 6), decided 2026-10-23: a provider without `_ROLE_CLAIM` runs as `APP`; an explicit `_ROLE_SOURCE=IDP` without a claim stops the start. The alternatives not taken: `IDP` applying the fixed `_ROLE` at every sign-in (an Admin promoted in the app demoted at their next sign-in, and nobody promotable in the app), and refusing to start until each provider names `_ROLE_SOURCE` (every existing instance stopped at the upgrade).
