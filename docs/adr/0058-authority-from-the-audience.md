A "connection" there is a system, a "job" or "thread" a conversation, a "work item" a conversation's subject, a "company agent" a trigger's runs and an "interactive agent" a person's conversation

## Context

- An agent's kind (`COMPANY` or `INTERACTIVE`, ADR-0049) was fixed for its life and decided five things at once: how work starts, whose credentials act, who pays for the model, who sees the result and whether the agent may ask. One specialist had to be hired twice, and each new need added a mechanism beside the switch: five account mechanisms per connection (the connection's token, the company OAuth account, agents' accounts, people's accounts, shared accounts) and ten kinds of credential.
- The agent row held its definition, its configuration, whose accounts act and who pays. The person who used an agent never counted, so per-person settings and per-person model keys were impossible.
- Credentials were resolved at every call through fallback chains, so a thread's identity could change while it ran, and repairs reached people who could not act on them.
- A connection was an event source, a tool provider, a repository host, an OAuth client and a credential holder; work was shaped like a tracker (a work item was an issue snapshot), so chats had no place.
- A first redesign answered the review of 2026-10-10 by adding concepts (profiles, compute pools, saved bindings, grants, consent records, a git gateway) on that foundation, which still kept company and personal work as two worlds. It was rolled back: too many concepts, the wrong foundation, and a confusing UX. The test for this design is fewer nouns.

## Decision

**What starts the work, by its audience, carries the authority.** Every conversation uses exactly one account per system, fixed for its whole life; what differs is who picks it.

1. **Principals.** Work acts for a principal: a **member** or a **service identity**, a non-human member that admins manage, which holds accounts and model access as a person does. Groups may be given the right to use a service identity.
2. **Personal and shared spaces.** In a personal space (the app, the person's AI client, a direct message to the chat bot) Tinfirm binds, at the first use of each system, the person's own account, else a grant to one of their groups, else asks them to connect; the person's own model access pays. In a shared space or a standing rule (a chat channel, a tracker event, a schedule, a webhook) the trigger's author picked an account per system and the model access, among those they are entitled to. A binding is checked at every use and never switches: a trigger whose author loses a right pauses.
3. **Systems and accounts.** A system is an external service registered once (Jira Data Center or Cloud, GitHub, GitLab, a git host, an MCP server, Slack, Teams, Buzz, a webhook) with one OAuth app, and holds no account of its own. An account is a principal's credential on a system, one per principal per system, and says who the principal is there. A grant lends a service identity's account to a group, `READ` or `WRITE`. Admins register company systems; members may register personal MCP servers, reachable only by runs that act as them, unless the company switches them off.
4. **Agents and settings.** An agent is a definition (instructions, the runtimes it is tested on, the systems, tools and repositories it may use, which actions ask), private to its creator until shared with groups, and holds no credentials. A person's settings and a trigger's settings narrow it (runtime and model among those allowed, extra instructions, tools off, defaults), never widen it, except for the person's own MCP servers.
5. **Triggers.** A trigger belongs to a system and holds its events and conditions (the event vocabulary of ADR-0043 stays) or, on a chat, its channels; the agent and instruction; an account per system; the model access; its team; and where results go. On a tracker its account polls and writes back, so it sees only what that account sees, and "assigned" and "mentioned" mean that account. Anyone who may use the agent creates triggers.
6. **Chats.** One Tinfirm bot per chat system, a service identity's account, carries messages only. A channel trigger answers members of the workspace (never guests or other organisations) with the trigger's accounts; a direct message is a personal conversation. Chat users are linked to members automatically when one identity provider vouches for both, else once by a sign-in link.
7. **Conversations.** The conversation is the one object of work: one agent, one place (a Tinfirm thread, a chat thread, a tracker issue, a scheduled firing), its accounts and model access, its turns, decisions and outputs. It may be about a subject; the work item goes. Conversations are independent and run in parallel, each with its own session and branch. A personal conversation publishes nothing by itself: it writes to a tracker only through write tools under their modes.
8. **Two decisions.** A question, answered by anyone who can write in the place; an approval (a gated action, a request for a repository or network host, the review of a result), decided by the person in a personal conversation and by the trigger's team in a shared one. An approved request covers the conversation, or the agent for good when the approver may edit the agent and chooses so.
9. **Tools.** Three layers that only narrow: each tool's effect (`READ` or `WRITE`; an MCP tool's from its hints, confirmed by an admin, unknown counting as `WRITE`), the agent's mode (`ALLOW` or `ASK`; an unlisted tool is unavailable), and the settings and the account (a `READ` grant refuses writes). Platform tools: `ask`, `request`, `save_output`, `list_repositories`, `finish`.
10. **Governance.** Three roles: OWNER, ADMIN, MEMBER. An agent is seen by its creator, the groups it is shared with and admins; a trigger and its conversations by its author, its team and admins; a personal conversation by its person and, in full for support and audit, the Owner, as the app says. Budgets per principal (ADR-0059). Kill switches: pause an agent, pause a trigger, suspend a principal. A member who leaves: their principal is kept as `LEFT`, their accounts and personal systems are deleted, their triggers pause until an admin reassigns them, their agents pass to the admins; one who rejoins gets a new principal.
11. **Build.** A fresh schema, no data migration; built package by package beside the running application: principals and systems; model access; agents, settings and conversations in the app; Jira triggers, then GitHub and GitLab; chats and the webhook; governance.

The design of record is [PLATFORM.md](../PLATFORM.md); the reasoning per decision is in the design record.

## Consequences

- One specialist serves triggers and people. The credential matrix becomes one rule, and every page can say who acts and who pays before work starts.
- What goes: agent kinds; `usage`; the connection's token, the company OAuth account and agents' accounts (now service identities' accounts); shared accounts and their fallback order (now grants, bound once); the tracker and repositories connection types; model credentials on agents; resolving credentials at every call; work items and one active run per issue; four decision kinds; group rights `VIEW`, `RUN`, `DECIDE` (now the trigger's team); tool levels, `DENY`, the `changeRequest` and `sourceWrite` policies and platform-tool switches.
- People without their own account on a system can still work through an explicit grant; nothing falls back silently.
- Shared-space triggers let anyone who may write there (a Jira comment, a channel message) drive the agent with the trigger's accounts, prompt injection included. Accepted, with mitigations: the editor says so and suggests narrowly scoped service identities; channels refuse guests and other organisations.
- The running application (the design of 2026-10-05, archived in [archive/v2/PLATFORM.md](../archive/v2/PLATFORM.md)) stays until the rebuild replaces it; [CONVENTIONS §14](../CONVENTIONS.md#14-the-restarts-domain) keeps describing it until each package rewrites its part.
- The runner protocol does not change; the runner host gains the model proxy (ADR-0059).

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the two kinds and add duplication or linked twins.** Two names and handles for one specialist, and the credential switch stays on the agent. Rejected.
- **Two profiles per agent, Automation and Conversation (the first redesign).** Kept company and personal as two worlds inside each agent and added compute pools, bindings and consent records around them. Rolled back.
- **One principal per trigger** (the trigger's account names one principal whose accounts act everywhere). Simpler, but the owner chose an account per system on the trigger, filled from one choice in the editor.
- **Triggers always act as a service identity, or always as the person who caused the event.** The owner chose: standing rules act with what their author picked; personal spaces act as the person; a shared channel never answers with one reader's permissions.
- **Strictly own accounts, no grants.** Clean, but analysts without access to a code host could not read code through an agent; an explicit grant per group was chosen instead of the old silent fallback.
- **Model keys granted to groups, or company keys as a fallback in conversations.** Rejected: a person's conversation is paid by the person's own model access; company model access appears on triggers.
