# ADR-0049: Company and interactive agents, groups with rights on agents, and people's own accounts on connections

- Status: Superseded by [ADR-0058](0058-authority-from-the-audience.md) on 2026-10-12 (was Proposed (the owner decided the points of the Decision on 2026-10-08))
- Date: 2026-10-08
- Sources: the owner's request of 2026-10-08 (an agent's tool calls carry the permissions of the person who gave the work, on every connection, the company's own MCP servers and the trackers alike; automated agents nobody steers, whose output only people with a specific permission see; that permission as a group assigned to people); PLATFORM.md §3 (principle 5), §4.1, §4.2, §4.6, §4.7, §4.8, §6, §7.2, §8; CONVENTIONS §6, §14.1, §14.5, §14.6, §14.7, §14.9, §14.10; [ADR-0007](0007-credentials-stay-in-deployment.md), [ADR-0035](0035-agent-usage-policy.md), [ADR-0038](0038-a-job-is-a-thread-with-an-agent.md), [ADR-0040](0040-mcp-tool-connections.md), [ADR-0045](0045-mcp-server-for-peoples-clients.md), [ADR-0046](0046-agent-tool-permissions.md), [ADR-0048](0048-jira-cloud-connection.md); the threat model (1.27, R33, 8.23, Q19)
- Changes: PLATFORM.md §4.1, where an agent has no fixed kind: it has one of two. §4.6 and ADR-0038, where a thread is between people and one agent: an interactive agent's thread is between one person and the agent. §4.2, §7.2 and ADR-0040, where every server-executed tool call runs with the connection's token: an interactive agent's runs with the person's own account. §4.7 and CONVENTIONS §14.1, where every member sees every work item and decides every decision their role allows: what a company agent produces is seen and decided by the groups that hold a right on it, and an interactive thread by its person. ADR-0048's later "3LO as a second credential" is a person's account of kind OAUTH.
- Amended by: [ADR-0051](0051-connection-company-account.md), under which Tinfirm obtains an OAuth grant for the company too, a connection's own account, not only for people (§6)
- Amended by: [ADR-0052](0052-shared-accounts.md), under which an interactive agent's call may use a shared account of the person's group (§7), and an interactive thread's workspace is cloned and pushed with the person's or the shared account (§4)

## Context

- Every server-executed tool call (a tracker's `get`, `search`, `label`; every tool of an MCP connection) runs with the connection's token, whoever gave the work (CONVENTIONS §14.10). Two members who ask the same agent the same question about Jira get the same answer, also the one who may not open the project in Jira. The threat model accepts this as R33 (1.27): a member reads, through an agent, at the breadth of the connection's token.
- Tinfirm cannot evaluate another system's permissions, and of an MCP server it knows only tool names and schemas. The one universal way to give a call the person's rights is to make the call as the person, with a credential that system issued to that person, and let the system enforce its own rules.
- The owner wants two kinds of specialist: automated ones that nobody can prompt, with the company's credentials, whose output only people with a specific permission see; and interactive ones that a person talks to, always with that person's own credentials. The permission is to be a group that one or more people are assigned to, not a list per agent.
- ADR-0045 made the rule for the other direction: a person's AI client acts as the member who authorized it, never with powers of its own. The agents' accounts on connections (`connection_account`) are the precedent for an identity per actor with its own secret.
- The MCP specification makes an MCP server over HTTP an OAuth 2.1 resource server (RFC 9728 protected-resource metadata, RFC 8414 or OpenID Connect discovery for the authorization server, PKCE, RFC 8707 resource indicators, Client ID Metadata Documents or dynamic registration or a pre-registered client). Tinfirm implements the server half for people's clients (ADR-0045); the client half is the same protocol from the other side, and the trackers have authorization servers of their own (Atlassian's 3LO, Jira Data Center's OAuth 2.0 provider, GitHub's and GitLab's OAuth applications).

## Decision

### 1. Two kinds of agent

`agent.kind` is `COMPANY` or `INTERACTIVE`, set when the agent is created and never changed (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED` on an update that names another kind).

| | `COMPANY` | `INTERACTIVE` |
|---|---|---|
| Starts work | Triggers only (events, schedules, and "run now" by a member with the `RUN` right). A trigger may name only a `COMPANY` agent (`400 INVALID_TRIGGER`) | A member, in Tinfirm or through their AI client: a conversation, a thread on a work item, a mention in a Tinfirm comment. Never a trigger |
| Mentions on the source (Jira, GitHub, GitLab) | Only through a `MENTIONED` or `COMMENTED` trigger, as a standing instruction: the built-in rule that a mention from the source starts or continues the agent's thread (PLATFORM.md §4.6, §4.8) no longer applies to any agent; a mention of a company agent without such a trigger does nothing | Never: the mention gets a `SYSTEM` note, as an `OWNER`-usage agent's does (ADR-0035) |
| Questions (`ask`) | Never: `policies.ask` is `DENY`, locked (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED` for `ALLOW`); a `BLOCKED` result keeps its open questions as text | As today |
| Other platform tools, policies, tool modes | As today (ADR-0046) | As today |
| Credential of its tool calls | The connection's token (today's rule) | The person's own account on the connection (§5), never the connection's token |
| Thread | The company's: its decisions are decided by the groups with `DECIDE` on the agent | One person's (§4) |
| Visible to | OWNER, and the members of the groups with `VIEW` on the agent (§3) | OWNER and the thread's person |
| `usage` | Kept on the row, ignored: `usableByMe` is false for everyone | As today (ADR-0035) |

`Agent.kind` is required on a create. The migration gives existing agents `INTERACTIVE`, and `COMPANY` to those a trigger names, setting their `policy_ask` to `DENY`.

### 2. Groups, and rights on company agents

A **group** is a named set of members of the company: `user_group` (`group` is a reserved word in SQL; the API says groups: `id`, `company_id`, `name` unique per company ignoring case, 1 to 100 characters, `description` up to 500, `created_at`, `updated_at`) and `user_group_member` (`company_id`, `group_id`, `user_id`, with a composite foreign key to `company_membership` `ON DELETE CASCADE`, so leaving the company leaves every group). Groups live in the `company` module. OWNER and ADMIN create, rename, fill and delete them (`MANAGE_GROUPS`); every member may list their names (`VIEW_GROUPS`, so that whoever edits an agent can pick one); the members of a group are shown with `MANAGE_GROUPS` only. API tag `Groups`: `GET` and `POST /api/companies/{companyId}/groups`, `GET`, `PUT` and `DELETE /api/companies/{companyId}/groups/{groupId}`; `PUT` replaces name, description and members as a whole; a member named twice counts once; a user who is not a member of the company is `400 VALIDATION_FAILED`. Deleting a group deletes its rights on agents. Audited as `group.created`, `group.updated` (the name, the members added and removed by id), `group.deleted`.

A **right** is what a group holds on one company agent: `agent_group_right` (`company_id`, `agent_id`, `group_id`, `right` in `VIEW`, `RUN`, `DECIDE`; primary key agent, group and right; cascades from both). `VIEW`: see the agent's threads, their comments, transcripts, decisions, documents and artifacts. `RUN`: run the agent's triggers now (with the role's `RUN_TRIGGERS`). `DECIDE`: decide the decisions of the agent's threads: access requests, approvals and reviews (with the role's `DECIDE`). `DECIDE` and `RUN` do not imply `VIEW`; the web app warns when a group gets one without it. OWNER holds every right on every agent implicitly; nobody else does, ADMIN included. Rights are set by whoever may change the agent (`MANAGE_AGENTS`, or `CREATE_AGENTS` for an agent one owns), as the rest of the agent is, with `PUT /api/companies/{companyId}/agents/{agentId}/group-rights` (`[{groupId, rights}]`, replaced as a whole, at most 50 groups), shown as `Agent.groupRights` (`[{groupId, groupName, rights}]`); on an `INTERACTIVE` agent any entry is `400 VALIDATION_FAILED`. Audited as `agent.group_rights.changed` (the whole list, by ids). The check is one query in the `agent` module, `AgentRights.holds(companyId, agentId, userId, right)` and `AgentRights.agentsWithRight(companyId, userId, right)`, through the `company` module's port `GroupMembership.groupsOf(companyId, userId)`; OWNER short-circuits to true.

### 3. Visibility

The rule, applied in the services the controllers and the MCP endpoint's tools call (never only in a controller), for the calling member:

- A **thread** (job) of a `COMPANY` agent is visible when the caller is OWNER or holds `VIEW` on the agent. A thread of an `INTERACTIVE` agent is visible when the caller is OWNER or the thread's person.
- A **work item** is visible when the caller is OWNER or may see at least one of its threads. (Every work item has a thread: a trigger, a mention or a person created it with one.)
- A **comment** with a `job_id` follows its thread; a comment without one (the work item's own conversation: people's comments at the top level, imported comments, SYSTEM notes) follows the work item. **Runs**, their events and transcripts, **decisions** and **artifacts** follow their thread; **attachments** follow the work item.
- What is not visible is `404 NOT_FOUND`, as for another company's id, never `403`; lists leave it out; the inbox counts only what the caller may see and decide. `list_work_items`, `list_jobs`, `list_decisions`, `list_comments`, the run endpoints and their event stream, the attachment download and every MCP read tool apply it.
- **Deciding** needs the role's `DECIDE` and, for a `COMPANY` thread, OWNER or the `DECIDE` right on its agent; for an `INTERACTIVE` thread, the thread's person only: OWNER sees the thread, ends it, cancels and retries its turns, and never replies or decides in it (`409 THREAD_BOUND`, as for any other member who sees it). An answer on the source (a reply on Jira) never answers an `INTERACTIVE` thread's question; a `COMPANY` agent asks none.
- **Run now** needs the role's `RUN_TRIGGERS` and OWNER or the `RUN` right on the trigger's agent.

A member's own view is computed once per request (`Visibility`: OWNER, the person's id, the agents with `VIEW`) and applied in the stores' queries. VIEWER keeps its role: it sees what the rule shows and decides nothing.

### 4. A thread of an interactive agent belongs to one person

`job.user_id` is the member whose message started the thread (null for a `COMPANY` agent's thread); `run.acting_user_id` copies it onto every run of the thread. Rules:

- Only the thread's person replies in it, answers its questions, approves its calls and access requests, ends it, cancels or retries its turns. OWNER may end it and cancel or retry its turns, never reply or decide. Anyone else who sees the thread and tries is `409 THREAD_BOUND`; who may not see it gets `404`.
- An interactive agent has at most one open thread **per person** per work item (today: per agent); a second person starting a thread with the same agent on the same work item starts their own. A mention of an interactive agent in a Tinfirm comment continues or starts the mentioning member's thread. Comments imported from the source never reach an interactive agent's threads, and replies on the source never answer their questions: the mirrored question says to reply in Tinfirm.
- **Before** a conversation or a thread starts, and before **every turn** of an interactive thread is requested (`Turns.request`), the person must hold an account (§5) on every connection the agent has a tool of (`agent_tool` rows with a level or an `ALLOW` or `ASK` entry). Otherwise `409 ACCOUNTS_REQUIRED`, with `details.connections` `[{id, name}]`, and the message *"You need to connect {names} with your credentials, and after that you can communicate with this agent."* `Agent.missingAccounts` lists them for the caller on an interactive agent, so the web app shows the hint before the person writes.
- `request_access` for a connection's tools is refused for an interactive agent (`TOOL_DENIED`, "whoever manages the agent gives it tools"); the tools of an interactive agent are its configuration. Repositories and network hosts are requested as today, decided by the thread's person.
- **Write-back** of an interactive thread on an issue or change request (its end-of-turn comment, its questions, the link to its change request) is written with the thread's person's account on the work item's connection, as the person, with the prefix `*{agent} (Tinfirm agent):*`; without such an account nothing is written and the outbox row is dropped with the reason. The trigger's actions (`STATUS_TO`, labels, `CLOSE`, `ATTACH_ARTIFACTS`) belong to triggers, so they never apply. Loop prevention stays by the ids of what Tinfirm wrote: the person's own comments on the source are imported as before.

### 5. A person's own account on a connection

A **person's account** is the person's credential on a connection of the company: `connection_person_account` (`company_id`, `connection_id`, `user_id`; `kind` `TOKEN` or `OAUTH`; `account_name`, the identity at the system when it is known, up to 255 characters; `secret_id`; `scopes` up to 1000 characters; `access_expires_at` for `OAUTH`; `api_base_url` up to 2000 characters, for `OAUTH` only: the address the account's token is valid at, when it is not the connection's (§6); `connected_at`, `last_used_at`, `last_error` up to 500 characters). Primary key connection and user; composite foreign keys to `connection` and to `company_membership`, both `ON DELETE CASCADE`, so leaving the company or deleting the connection deletes the account; RLS as every tenant table. The secret is a vault secret of the company **owned by the person**: `secret.owner_user_id`; an owned secret is never listed, revealed or changed through the Secrets API or page, it is revealed only for a call that acts as its owner, and it is deleted with the account. Its value is the token as the system takes it (a Jira Data Center PAT; a Jira Cloud `email:api-token`; a GitHub or GitLab token; an MCP server's token, sent in the connection's `authHeader` as the connection's own token is) or, for `OAUTH`, a JSON object `{"accessToken","refreshToken","tokenType","expiresAt","scope"}`. A `TOKEN` account is sent as the connection's own token is; an `OAUTH` token is sent as `Authorization: Bearer` by every client, whatever the connection's own scheme or `authHeader` (Jira Cloud's Basic, GitLab's `PRIVATE-TOKEN` and an MCP server's custom header do not apply to it).

Every connection with tools takes accounts: `JIRA_DC`, `JIRA_CLOUD`, `GITHUB`, `GITLAB` and `MCP`; `GIT` and `SCHEDULE` do not (`400`). A person connects one of two ways:

- **A token** the person stores: `PUT /api/companies/{companyId}/me/accounts/{connectionId}/token` with `{token}`. The server checks it with the kind's "who am I" (`whoAmI`: Jira's `myself`, GitHub's `user`, GitLab's `user`; it fills `account_name`) and, for an MCP server, with `initialize` and `tools/list`, and refuses a token the system refuses (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED` with the system's status, never the token).
- **OAuth**, when the connection offers it (§6): `POST /api/companies/{companyId}/me/accounts/{connectionId}/authorize` answers `{url}` to open; the browser comes back to `GET /api/me/accounts/callback`.

`GET /api/companies/{companyId}/me/accounts` lists, for the caller, every connection that takes accounts with the methods it offers (`TOKEN`, and `OAUTH` when configured) and the caller's account on it (`NOT_CONNECTED`, `CONNECTED`, `ERROR` with `lastError`; `accountName`, `scopes`, `connectedAt`, `lastUsedAt`); `DELETE …/me/accounts/{connectionId}` disconnects (revoking the OAuth token at the system when it has a revocation endpoint). A person sees and changes only their own; owners and admins see on the Members page whether a member has an account on a connection, never the account. Audited as `account.connected` (the connection, the kind, never the token), `account.disconnected`, `account.refresh_failed` once per failure streak, and `account.connect_failed` when an OAuth grant cannot be used and no account is kept (§6); `secret.accessed` keeps its purposes (`run-tool:{runId}`, `decision-tool:{decisionId}`, `connection-write:{id}`) and gains `userId` for an owned secret.

### 6. OAuth: Tinfirm as a client

A connection may offer OAuth to people. For an `MCP` connection the server **discovers** it as the MCP specification says: a `POST` to the endpoint without a token answers `401` with `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer resource_metadata="…"` (else the server tries `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource{path}`), the protected-resource metadata names the authorization servers, whose metadata (`/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server`, with the path-insertion rules of RFC 8414, else `/.well-known/openid-configuration`) names the endpoints; `code_challenge_methods_supported` must list `S256`, else the connection offers no OAuth (`toolsReadError`-style, shown on the page). The discovery runs on create and on "Read tools", through the outbound client and its guard, and its result is stored on the connection (`oauth_authorization_endpoint`, `oauth_token_endpoint`, `oauth_revocation_endpoint`, `oauth_registration_endpoint`, `oauth_scopes_supported`, `oauth_cimd_supported`). For a tracker the endpoints are a fixed **profile per kind**: Jira Cloud's 3LO (`https://auth.atlassian.com/authorize` with `audience=api.atlassian.com` and `prompt=consent`, `https://auth.atlassian.com/oauth/token`, scopes `read:jira-work read:jira-user write:jira-work offline_access read:me`, the account from `https://api.atlassian.com/me`, which `read:me` allows; without it connecting still works and the name stays empty; the API at Atlassian's gateway for the site, `https://api.atlassian.com/ex/jira/{cloudId}`, where alone a 3LO token is valid, REST API v3 under it, while issue links stay on the site's URL), Jira Data Center's provider (`{base}/rest/oauth2/latest/authorize` and `/token`, scope `WRITE`, PKCE; the account from `/rest/api/2/myself`), GitHub's OAuth application (`https://{host}/login/oauth/authorize` and `/access_token`, scope `repo`; the account from `/user`), GitLab's (`{origin}/oauth/authorize` and `/oauth/token`, scope `api`, PKCE; the account from `/api/v4/user`).

Tinfirm's **identity as a client**, in this order: its own Client ID Metadata Document, published at `{TINFIRM_PUBLIC_URL}/oauth/client-metadata.json` (`client_id` equal to that URL, `client_name` "Tinfirm", `redirect_uris` with the callback, `token_endpoint_auth_method` `none`), where the authorization server advertises `client_id_metadata_document_supported`; else dynamic registration (RFC 7591) at the `registration_endpoint`, once per connection, the issued client id and secret kept on the connection (`oauth_client_id`, `oauth_client_secret_id` in the vault); else the client an owner or admin registered at the system and stored on the connection (`Connection.oauth`: `clientId`, `clientSecret` write-only as a vault secret, optional `scopes`; the trackers always need this). The connection's page shows the callback to register and the state of the discovery.

The **flow**: the authorize request carries `response_type=code`, the client id, the callback, PKCE `S256`, a `state` of 32 random bytes bound to the person's session and the connection (kept with the code verifier in `oauth_authorization_request`, an instance-level table, for 10 minutes), the scopes, and `resource` (RFC 8707) with the MCP endpoint for an `MCP` connection. The callback (`GET /api/me/accounts/callback?code&state`, a session is required, the `state` must belong to it) exchanges the code at the token endpoint through the outbound client and its guard, reads the account name where the profile says, stores the tokens in the person's secret, and redirects to the web app's accounts page with `?connected={connectionId}` or `?error={code}`. For Jira Cloud the callback also resolves the gateway for the connection's site and keeps it on the account (`api_base_url`): the connection's base URL when it already is the gateway form; else the entry of `GET https://api.atlassian.com/oauth/token/accessible-resources`, called with the person's token, whose `url` is the site's (ignoring case and a trailing slash), or the single entry for a site that is not an `*.atlassian.net` host; the result passes the outbound guard. When the grant names no such site, or the lookup fails, no account is kept: the callback redirects with `?error=site_not_granted` and the audit records `account.connect_failed`. **Refresh**: a call that finds the access token expired, or expiring within the tool timeout, refreshes it first, outside the checking transaction, through the guard, and stores the new tokens in a transaction of its own; a refresh that fails sets `last_error`, answers the call `CREDENTIAL_REQUIRED`, and is audited once. Every authorization-server host is checked by the outbound guard like a base URL (https, no redirects, no private addresses unless the operator allows them).

### 7. The call path

`ConnectionCredentials` gains `callCredential(companyId, connectionId, kind, actingUserId, purpose)`, a sealed result that follows the agent's kind, not the acting user alone: `Service(token)` (a company agent's call, whatever the run's acting user; or an MCP connection without a secret: `None`), `Person(userId, accountName, token)` (an interactive agent's call: the person's account, which also says whether it is a `TOKEN` or an `OAUTH` account and the address its token is valid at), or `PersonRequired(reason)` (an interactive agent whose person has no usable account). `ToolCalls` keeps its order (the run; the tool; the agent's mode, `TOOL_DENIED` and `NO_ACCESS` as today; the arguments) and then asks for the credential with `run.acting_user_id`: `PersonRequired` answers the request `REJECTED` with the new code **`CREDENTIAL_REQUIRED`** and a message the agent relays (*"`jira_search` runs as the person you work for, and they have no account on `Acme Jira` connected to Tinfirm. Ask them to connect one under My accounts; never ask them for a token."*). `ToolApprovals` does the same with the decision's run. A `Person` credential marks the account's `last_used_at`, and a client sends an `OAUTH` token as `Authorization: Bearer` whatever the connection's own scheme (§5). The `tool.called` audit event gains `actingUserId` and `credential` (`SERVICE` or `PERSON`). An interactive agent's tool descriptions end with "Runs as the person you work for, with their own permissions at the system." Nothing in `run.assign` or on the runner host changes.

### 8. Web

- **Groups**, a page beside Members (OWNER and ADMIN): the groups with their members; create, rename, fill, delete.
- **Agents**: the kind on the list and the glance; the form takes the kind on create and shows it read-only afterwards; a `COMPANY` agent's form has a **Rights** tab (groups with View, Run and Decide; a warning for Run or Decide without View) and no `ask` switch; an `INTERACTIVE` agent's "Talk to" shows the missing accounts with a link to **My accounts** instead of the composer.
- **My accounts**, a page of the company shell for every member: each connection that takes accounts, with Connect (OAuth) or Add a token, the state, the account name, Disconnect.
- **Connections**: the OAuth settings of a connection (the discovery's state for an MCP server, the client id and secret, the callback to register).
- **Members**: per member, the connections they hold an account on.
- Threads, decisions and runs the caller may not see are absent; a thread of another person shows no composer; the run page names the acting person.

### 9. Threat model and audit

A new asset, people's accounts on connections (OAuth refresh tokens and personal tokens of members for the company's systems: acting as that person at the system within the scopes, until revoked), in the company's vault, owned by the person, revealed per call, deleted with the membership or the connection, revoked on My accounts. TB8 gains the authorization servers of connections. New rows: a prompt-injected interactive agent acting as its person with the person's scopes (bounded by what the person may do; writes stay behind the agent's tool modes and `ASK`); a forged or replayed callback (`state` bound to the session, PKCE, 10 minutes); the discovery of an MCP server pointing the token request at an internal address (the guard). Row 1.27 and R33 narrow to company agents, whose threads only their groups see; Q19 stays for them. Groups are a new permission unit beside the roles (CONVENTIONS §6). Audit actions: `group.created`, `group.updated`, `group.deleted`, `agent.group_rights.changed`, `account.connected`, `account.connect_failed`, `account.disconnected`, `account.refresh_failed`, `connection.oauth.changed`; `agent.created` and `agent.updated` record the kind.

## Consequences

- The owner's example holds: two members asking the same interactive agent about Jira get Jira's answer for each of them, and a member without an account on the connection is told to connect one before the agent works for them. On the company's own MCP servers and on the trackers alike.
- A company agent is a closed box: triggers in, output to its groups, no steering beyond its access requests, which its groups decide. What it writes to Jira or another system with the company's credentials is visible there by that system's rules; in Tinfirm by the groups.
- The visibility rule touches every read path of the work and run modules and the MCP endpoint's tools; a rights test, built like the tenant isolation test, calls each one as a member outside the group and as another person.
- Tinfirm becomes an OAuth client, with a client metadata document of its own, a callback, and tokens of people in the vault: the operations guide names the callback to register and the scopes to ask for (reads first).
- Nothing changes on the runner host or in the protocol; the deployment contract holds (new tables and paths, one public callback and one public metadata document, no new port or variable).
- Later: group membership from a company identity provider's claims; groups as the usage policy of interactive agents; the enterprise-managed authorization profile of the MCP specification (identity assertion grants and token exchange at the company's identity provider) as a third way to an account; an identity assertion for the company's own servers.

## Alternatives considered

- **One connection per person** (ADR-0040's workaround). Rejected: *n* connections, *n* tool prefixes, *n* grants per agent.
- **Keep the service token and ask the system whether the person may see each result.** Not universal (an MCP server has no such API; Jira's needs an administrator), and it still fetches what the person may not see. Rejected.
- **Forward the person's Tinfirm token** (their AI client's grant) to the connection. Its audience is Tinfirm, the MCP specification forbids token passthrough, and a member in the web app has no token. Rejected.
- **A credential mode per connection and an acting person per turn**, one agent in both modes. Rejected for the two kinds: simpler to reason about, nothing falls back silently.
- **A list of people per agent** instead of groups. Rejected by the owner: a group is assigned to people and reused across agents.
- **ADMIN with implicit rights.** Rejected by the owner: a specific permission means a group.
- **Interactive threads visible to everyone, as today.** Rejected by the owner: the thread is the person's, OWNER sees it for support and audit.
- **No write-back from interactive threads.** Rejected by the owner: the comment is written as the person, with the person's account, or not at all.
