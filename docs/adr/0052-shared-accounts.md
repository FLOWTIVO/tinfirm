# ADR-0052: Shared accounts: a company credential on a connection, granted to groups, for interactive agents

- Status: Superseded by [ADR-0058](0058-authority-from-the-audience.md) on 2026-10-12 (was Accepted (the owner asked for it on 2026-10-08))
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Sources: [ADR-0049](0049-agent-kinds-groups-and-personal-accounts.md) §2, §4 to §7 (groups, people's accounts, Tinfirm as an OAuth client, the call path), [ADR-0050](0050-connection-types.md) (the types; a repositories connection holds the git token), [ADR-0051](0051-connection-company-account.md) (the company's own OAuth account; `oauth_authorization_request.subject`); PLATFORM.md §4.1, §4.2, §4.6, §7.2; CONVENTIONS §14.1, §14.5, §14.13; threat model 8.24, 8.27, 8.28, R27
- Changes: ADR-0049 §7, where an interactive agent's call has its person's own account or nothing: it may have a shared account of the person's group. ADR-0049 §4 and `run.assign`, where an interactive thread's workspace is cloned and pushed with the repositories connection's token: it is the person's or the shared account's. ADR-0050, where a repositories connection has no accounts of people: it takes people's and shared accounts for the interactive agents' workspaces. ADR-0051's "not in this decision": a company grant on a tracker exists now, as a shared account, used by interactive agents only.

## Context

- Since ADR-0049 an interactive agent's tool call runs with its person's own account on the connection, never with the connection's token, so a member reads and writes no more than they may at the system. The repositories of the thread's workspace are the exception: `run.assign` carries the repositories connection's git token for every repository the agent has access to (ADR-0050), the runner host clones and pushes with it, and change requests are opened with it. A member with no access at the code host reads and writes every repository of the agent through the agent, as the connection's service account.
- A company may want some of that on purpose. Its business analysts, for example, hold no access at the code host and should still ask an interactive agent about the code. That is a company decision about a group, not something each analyst can bring: they have no token to connect. The company wants to hand the group one credential, read-only or scoped at the system, and keep the company agents on the connection's own token. A read & write credential for another group must be possible the same way.
- ADR-0049 §2 gives the company groups with rights on company agents. ADR-0051 makes a grant the company obtains at an authorization server a company secret, with `oauth_authorization_request.subject` saying whom a pending authorization is for, and `PersonOAuth` the client of both subjects. ADR-0050 gives the git token its own connection. What is missing is a company-held account on a connection that a group's members act with, where they hold none of their own.

## Decision

**A connection may hold shared accounts: company credentials, each a token or an OAuth grant, read-only or read & write, granted to groups. An interactive agent acting for a person who holds no account of their own on the connection acts with the shared account of the person's group. The repositories of an interactive thread are cloned, pushed and their change requests opened with the person's or the shared account on the repositories connection, never with the connection's token. The connection's own token, and the company account of ADR-0051, stay the credential of the company agents, the poller, the catalogue read and the write-back of company threads; a shared account is never used for those.**

### 1. The account

`connection_shared_account` (`id`, `company_id`, `connection_id`, `name` unique per connection, `access` `READ` or `WRITE`, `kind` `TOKEN` or `OAUTH`, `secret_id` of a company secret, `account_name` when the system names it, `api_base_url` as a person's account has it (Jira Cloud's gateway), `scopes`, `access_expires_at`, `connected_at`, `connected_by_user_id`, `last_used_at`, `last_error`, `created_at`, `created_by_user_id`), with RLS as every tenant table, deleted with the connection, and `connection_shared_account_group` (`company_id`, `connection_id` copied from the account, `shared_account_id`, `group_id`; unique on group and connection, so the database keeps the rule that a group holds one account per connection), deleted with the account or the group (`V027__shared_accounts.sql`). A `TRACKER`, a `REPOSITORIES` and an `MCP` connection take shared accounts; `SCHEDULE` takes none (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED`).

- A **`TOKEN`** account names a company secret the admin stored on the Secrets page, as the connection's own token is named: a Jira Data Center PAT, a Jira Cloud `email:api-token`, a GitHub or GitLab token, a git token, an MCP server's token. The secret is checked as a person's stored token is (the kind's "who am I", which fills `account_name`; an MCP server's `initialize` and `tools/list`; a git host's `ls-remote` of the connection's first repository), and a secret the system refuses is `400 VALIDATION_FAILED` with the system's status, never the token. Rotation is a replace of that secret on the Secrets page; the account does not change. The secret is sent as the connection's own is (ADR-0049 §7).
- An **`OAUTH`** account is a grant an owner or admin obtains by signing in at the system's authorization server as the account the group should act as, exactly as the company account of ADR-0051: `POST …/connections/{connectionId}/shared-accounts/{id}/authorize` builds the authorization URL (the connection's client identity, PKCE `S256`, the state bound to the caller's session, the scopes, `resource`; `400 VALIDATION_FAILED` when the connection offers no OAuth), the request is stored with the subject `SHARED` and the account's id, the one callback `GET /api/me/accounts/callback` exchanges the code, reads the account name from the profile where there is one (Jira Cloud's `me`, GitHub's `user`, GitLab's `user`), stores the tokens as the company secret `CONNECTION_SHARED_ACCOUNT` (the grant as a JSON object, as ADR-0051 stores it; a second one is `_2`, `_3`, … as the vault names it), audits `connection.shared_account.connected` (`sharedAccountId`, `userId` of who signed in, `replaced`) and returns to the connection's page, `…/connections/{connectionId}?connected=shared-account&account={id}`, or with `?error={code}` (CONVENTIONS §14.13). The access token is refreshed before a call that would outlive it, as a person's and the company's are (`PersonOAuth`, one refresh at a time, a transient failure leaving the token, a refusal setting `last_error` and the status `ERROR`, audited once per streak as `connection.shared_account.refresh_failed`). An `OAUTH` account's token is always `Authorization: Bearer`, at its `api_base_url` when it has one. An account in `ERROR` is not fallen back from: the call fails as §2 says until someone signs in again.
- **Status**: `NOT_CONNECTED` (an `OAUTH` account created and not yet signed in), `CONNECTED`, `ERROR` with `lastError`. A `TOKEN` account is `CONNECTED` while its secret exists; a deleted secret makes it `ERROR` ("the secret was deleted").
- **Groups.** An account is granted to zero or more groups. A group's members act with it as §2 says. The same group may hold accounts on several connections; on one connection a group holds at most one account (`400 VALIDATION_FAILED`, "the group already holds the account `…` on this connection").

### 2. Who acts with a shared account

`ConnectionCredentials.callCredential(companyId, connectionId, agentKind, actingUserId, purpose)` resolves an `INTERACTIVE` agent's call in this order, and nothing changes for a `COMPANY` agent's:

1. the person's own account on the connection (ADR-0049 §5), as today: when the person holds one, it is the result, usable or not (an account in error is `PersonRequired` with `RECONNECT`, never a fall-through to a shared one);
2. else, when the person holds no account of their own there, the shared accounts of the connection granted to a group the person is in (`GroupMembership.groupsOf`): the `WRITE` ones before the `READ` ones, then the earlier `created_at`; a `NOT_CONNECTED` or `ERROR` account is skipped only when another usable one exists, else it is the result and the call fails with its reason;
3. else `PersonRequired`, as today: `CREDENTIAL_REQUIRED` for a tool call, the write dropped, the thread not started.

The result is a new `CallCredential.Shared(sharedAccountId, name, access, kind, token, accessExpiresAt, apiBaseUrl)`: `person()` is false, `shared()` true, and a client sends an `OAUTH` one as `Authorization: Bearer` whatever the connection's scheme. `tool.called` records `credential` `SHARED` with `sharedAccountId` and `sharedAccountName` beside `actingUserId`; `secret.accessed` keeps its purposes. The account's `last_used_at` is marked per call. The connection's own token is still never used for an interactive agent.

The membership is read per call, so leaving the group, losing the grant or the account's deletion take effect at the next call. A run keeps the git credential the runner host was assigned until it ends (§4).

### 3. READ is enforced by Tinfirm, not only by the token

A `READ` account's token should be scoped read-only at the system, and Tinfirm refuses writes with it in any case:

- the workspace of a run that clones with it is **read-only**: `run.assign` marks the repository `readOnly`, the host pushes nothing, no change request is opened and `changeRequest` stays unrequested;
- a tracker's `label` and every tool the catalogue or ADR-0046 classifies with **write effect** is refused with `TOOL_DENIED` and the message "`jira_label` writes, and you act through the read-only shared account `Analysts` on `Acme Jira`"; a `request_access` for such a tool is not raised;
- the **write-back** of the thread (its end-of-turn comment, its questions, the link to its change request) is dropped with the reason "the shared account is read-only", as a thread without an account drops it (ADR-0049 §4).

A `WRITE` account behaves as the person's own account does: the workspace follows the agent's access, the host pushes, change requests are opened with it, write tools run, and the write-back is written with it under the prefix `*{agent} (Tinfirm agent) for {person}:*`, since the system shows the shared identity. Commits of an interactive run name the person in the author, `{Person} via Tinfirm agent {agent} <agent+{id}@tinfirm.invalid>`, for a person's own account and a shared one alike.

### 4. The repositories of an interactive thread

`run.assign` for a run of an `INTERACTIVE` agent reveals, per repository of the workspace, the git token of the person's own account or the shared account on the repository's connection (the `REPOSITORIES` connection of ADR-0050), resolved as §2 and audited as `secret.accessed` with purpose `run:<runId>`; the `readOnly` flag of a `READ` account overrides the agent's level. `ChangeRequestOpener` and the related-change-request rewrites reveal the same way for such a run, and a `READ` account opens none. A `COMPANY` agent's run keeps the connection's token, as today. The runner host and the protocol do not change: the git credential of a workspace is a username and a token, which never enter the sandbox.

So a `REPOSITORIES` connection takes **people's accounts** (`TOKEN`; `OAUTH` on `GITHUB` and `GITLAB`, whose profiles exist; a `GIT` host takes a token only) and shared accounts, which changes ADR-0050's table: its "no accounts of people" becomes "no accounts of agents". My accounts lists repositories connections with a hint of what the token must hold (on GitHub Contents read, and Pull requests for a thread that opens change requests; on GitLab `read_repository` or `write_repository` and `api`).

**Before a thread starts and before every turn** (ADR-0049 §4), the person must hold an account, their own or a shared one, on every connection the agent has a tool of **and on the connection of every repository of the agent's access**. Otherwise `409 ACCOUNTS_REQUIRED` with `details.connections` `[{id, name, reason}]`, where `reason` is `NONE` or `RECONNECT` (the person's own account, or the shared account, must be signed in again) and the message names them: *"You need to connect Acme GitHub repositories with your credentials, and after that you can communicate with this agent."* `Agent.missingAccounts` lists them for the caller; an account the person holds through a group is shown on the agent page as "via Analysts (read-only)".

### 5. Management

- `GET`/`POST /api/companies/{companyId}/connections/{connectionId}/shared-accounts` (OWNER, ADMIN; `MANAGE_CONNECTIONS`), `PATCH`/`DELETE …/shared-accounts/{id}` (name, access, groups; `secretId` on a `TOKEN` account), `POST …/shared-accounts/{id}/authorize` (`OAUTH`; a browser session), `POST …/shared-accounts/{id}/test` (the check of §1 with the account's credential). `DELETE` revokes an `OAUTH` grant at the revocation endpoint where there is one, deletes the account's `OAUTH` secret (a `TOKEN` account's secret is the company's and stays), and audits `connection.shared_account.deleted`. Creating, updating and granting audit `connection.shared_account.created` and `.updated` (`groups`, `access`).
- `Connection.sharedAccounts` (`SharedAccount`: `id`, `name`, `access`, `kind`, `status`, `lastError`, `accountName`, `secretId`, `groups` `[{id, name}]`, `scopes`, `connectedAt`, `connectedByName`, `lastUsedAt`); `Group.sharedAccounts` `[{connectionId, connectionName, accountId, accountName, access}]` read-only on the Groups page. `Member` (ADR-0049 §8) gains `groupAccountConnectionIds`, the connections the member acts on through a group's shared account without an account of their own, beside `accountConnectionIds`.
- **Secrets page.** A `TOKEN` account's secret is an ordinary company secret, stored first. An `OAUTH` account's secret, `CONNECTION_SHARED_ACCOUNT`, is listed too, as the company account's of ADR-0051 is. `SecretMeta` gains `managed` (`CONNECTION_COMPANY_ACCOUNT`, `CONNECTION_SHARED_ACCOUNT`, the OAuth client secrets of ADR-0049 §6): the page shows what maintains it, offers no replace (a pasted value would break the grant) and keeps delete, which disconnects the account. The Secrets API refuses a replace of a managed secret with `400 VALIDATION_FAILED`.
- **Web.** A connection's page gets a **Shared accounts** section (trackers, repositories, MCP servers): one row per account with its name, access, kind, status, the groups, and for owners and admins **Add**, **Edit**, **Sign in with OAuth** or **Sign in again**, **Test** and **Remove**. The add dialog asks the name, the access, the credential (a company secret, or "sign in with OAuth" where the connection offers it) and the groups. The page handles the callback's return. The Groups page lists a group's shared accounts with links. The agent page's accounts hint and the thread's `ACCOUNTS_REQUIRED` message name shared accounts as §4 says.

### 6. Threat model and audit

- A shared account is a company asset beside A15 and A25: whoever reaches the server's database and vault key acts as it at the system, within its scope, until it is revoked or rotated. Its token is revealed per call, sent to a runner host only as a workspace's git credential (host only, never the sandbox), and redacted as every credential of a run is.
- A group grant is the company's decision to let the group's members act as the shared identity: its audit (`connection.shared_account.updated` with `groups`) and `tool.called` with `SHARED` and `actingUserId` keep who did what. At the system everything appears as the shared account; the write-back prefix and the commit author name the person.
- A `READ` account bounds what a prompt-injected or steered agent can do to reads, in Tinfirm and, when the token is scoped, at the system (8.24 extended). A `WRITE` account's scope is the token's, so the guide asks for one scoped to the repositories or projects the group should reach.

## Consequences

- The case this serves: a `REPOSITORIES` connection holds the company agents' git token and a read-only shared account `Analysts`, granted to the Analysts group; an analyst asks the interactive agent about the code, the host clones with the shared token, pushes nothing, and the analyst's tool calls at Jira run with their own Jira account. Another group gets a `WRITE` account on the same connection, scoped at GitHub to its repositories.
- The repository gap closes: an interactive thread never clones, pushes or opens a change request with the connection's token. A member with no account on the repositories connection and no group with one cannot start the thread, and is told what to connect.
- Nothing changes for company agents, the poller, the catalogue read, the write-back of company threads, the runner host or the protocol.
- Not in this decision: a person's own model credential or budget, network grants on interactive threads, ending a removed member's threads, a company grant that company agents use on a tracker (ADR-0051's "later"), group membership from an identity provider.

## Alternatives considered

- **A second token on the connection, used by every interactive agent.** One credential for all members, no groups: every member would act as it, and a developer with their own access would lose their own permissions. Rejected; groups are the unit the owner chose in ADR-0049.
- **People's accounts only.** The analysts hold no token to connect, and the company does not want to give them access at the code host. Rejected.
- **Shared accounts for company agents too.** They have the connection's token and the company account of ADR-0051; a second company credential for them is a connection of its own (ADR-0050). Rejected as out of scope.
- **A shared account before the person's own.** A company might want a group to act as the shared identity even where a member has an account. Rejected for now: the person's own permissions are the rule of ADR-0049, and a company that wants the shared one can leave the person's account unconnected.
